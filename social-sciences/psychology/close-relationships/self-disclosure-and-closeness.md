# Self-disclosure and closeness

## Summary

Closeness grows when two people gradually share more personal information and each responds with care. Aron and colleagues (1997) turned this into a laboratory procedure: pairs of strangers answered 36 questions that became more personal step by step, and the pairs felt closer than pairs who made small talk. The procedure shows that closeness can be built by structured, reciprocal disclosure. Privacy is the counterpart: people choose what to share, with whom and when.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Self-disclosure* is the sharing of personal information, thoughts and feelings with another person. The social penetration theory of Altman and Taylor (1973) says that relationships develop as disclosure becomes broader and deeper, and that the process needs reciprocity. In the procedure of Aron and colleagues (1997), pairs worked through three sets of questions over 45 minutes, moving from mild ones (for example, whom would you invite to dinner) to personal ones (for example, a memory of a difficult moment). After the task, pairs reported greater closeness than pairs given small talk questions.

### Analogy

Closeness is built like a bridge from both banks at once. If only one side builds, there is nothing to meet. The limit of the analogy is that disclosure can also be rejected, and trust must be present for each step.

### Privacy

People also need boundaries. Communication privacy management theory (Petronio, 2002) says that people treat private information as theirs, set rules for who may know it, and feel a breach of the rules as a violation. A useful rule: share at the pace that trust is built, and keep to oneself what one is not ready to share.

## Worked example

Two colleagues who want to build a friendship use a graded sequence.

| Stage | Example prompt | Depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is a skill you would like to have? | Low |
| 2 | What was the best part of your week? | Low to medium |
| 3 | What is something you are proud of that few people know? | Medium |
| 4 | What is a difficult moment that changed you? | High |

Each person answers each prompt and listens to the other. The stages follow the principle of the 1997 procedure: step up in depth, give turns and respond with attention. The prompts are examples and are not the published questions.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Structured disclosure works for building new friendships and for couples who want to reconnect. It is not a trick for creating romantic love on demand, and effects in the original study were short-term and measured right after the session. Over-disclosure too early can push people away, and some people are more cautious. Respect each person's limits.

## Common mistakes

- **Sharing too much too soon.** Depth should follow trust.
- **Disclosing without listening.** Responsiveness is the active ingredient.
- **Treating disclosure as manipulation.** Honest sharing is the point.
