# Oregano oil

## Summary

Oregano oil is an essential oil from Origanum vulgare and related species, rich in carvacrol and thymol. These phenols damage the cell membranes of fungi and bacteria in laboratory studies, including Candida albicans. It is sold as capsules, drops and essential oil. Human trials for fungal or bacterial infections are few and small. Undiluted oil irritates the skin and mucous membranes.

*This note is educational. It is not medical advice and does not replace a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist, especially during pregnancy, illness or when taking medicines.*

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Oregano oil* is the steam-distilled essential oil of oregano. *Carvacrol* and *thymol* are its main monoterpene phenols.

### Analogy

Carvacrol and thymol are like detergents for microbial membranes: they dissolve into the membrane and make it leak until the cell dies. The analogy breaks because the concentrations that kill microbes in a dish are hard to reach in the body without harming our own tissues.

### Mechanism

Carvacrol and thymol penetrate the lipid membrane of fungi and bacteria, disrupt it and cause leakage of ions and ATP. They also inhibit biofilm formation in the laboratory. Oral oil is absorbed and metabolized quickly.

### Proposed effects and evidence

Each entry states the proposed effect and how strong the human evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Antifungal action, including against Candida albicans.** Laboratory studies. Evidence: very limited.
- **Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory action.** Laboratory and animal studies; one small trial on intestinal parasites. Evidence: very limited.

## Worked example

A person with recurrent vaginal yeast infections reads that oregano oil kills Candida. Laboratory studies support this, but there are no reliable human trials, and oregano oil applied to mucous membranes causes burns. Antifungal medicines prescribed by a doctor are the evidence-based option.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Oregano as a culinary herb is safe. Concentrated oil can cause irritation, allergic reactions and digestive upset, and may interact with anticoagulants.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying undiluted oil to skin or mucosa.** It can cause burns.
- **Treating infections only with oregano oil.** Human evidence is lacking.
