# Eucalyptus oil

## Summary

Eucalyptus essential oil comes from Eucalyptus globulus. It is used for respiratory infections, muscle pain and inflammation. Its main compounds are 1,8-cineole (eucalyptol). Eucalyptol is anti-inflammatory and mucolytic, which may ease symptoms of colds and flu and muscle pain. Human evidence is summarized below; most claims rest on laboratory or small studies.

*This note is educational. It is not medical advice and does not replace a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist, especially during pregnancy, illness or when taking medicines.*

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Eucalyptus oil* is the essential oil of *Eucalyptus globulus*. Its characteristic compounds are 1,8-cineole (eucalyptol).

### Analogy

Eucalyptus oil is like opening a window in a stuffy room: the strong scent makes the nose feel clearer. The analogy breaks because the "clearer" feeling is mostly sensory; measurable airflow changes are small.

### Mechanism

**Uses described in the notes:** respiratory infections, muscle pain and inflammation.

**Why it is thought to work:** Eucalyptol is anti-inflammatory and mucolytic, which may ease symptoms of colds and flu and muscle pain.

Like other essential oils, it acts through smell (olfactory and limbic pathways) when inhaled and through local effects on skin and microbes when applied diluted ([Essential oils overview](essential-oils-overview.md)).

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the proposed effect and how strong the human evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Respiratory infections and cold symptoms.** Oral cineole capsules reduced exacerbations in COPD and sinusitis symptoms in trials; inhalation has limited evidence. Evidence: limited.
- **Muscle pain relief.** Used in rubs; small studies. Evidence: limited.
- **Anti-inflammatory action.** Laboratory evidence. Evidence: limited.

## Worked example

In a 6-month trial (Worth and colleagues, 2009), 242 people with COPD took 200 mg cineole capsules three times daily or placebo during winter in addition to usual treatment. Exacerbations were fewer with cineole. This standardized drug differs from home inhalation of eucalyptus oil.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Eucalyptus oil is highly toxic if swallowed, especially by children (a teaspoon can be dangerous). Avoid near the faces of young children.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving bottles within reach of children.** Ingestion causes serious poisoning.

## Practice
