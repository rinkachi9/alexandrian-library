# High-intensity interval training (HIIT)

## Summary

High-intensity interval training (HIIT) alternates short bouts of very hard exercise with periods of rest or easy exercise. Typical protocols use work bouts from about 20 seconds to 4 minutes at 80 to 95 percent of maximal heart rate or higher. HIIT improves maximal oxygen uptake (VO2 max) at least as much as moderate continuous training in less time, and it is used in sport, fitness and cardiac rehabilitation.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*High-intensity interval training* is training made of repeated high-intensity efforts separated by recovery periods. *Sprint interval training* (SIT) is a subtype with all-out efforts, such as 30-second sprints.

### Analogy

HIIT is like boiling water in a kettle in short bursts at full power instead of heating it slowly on a low flame. You reach a high temperature in less time. The analogy breaks because the body also adapts during the rest periods, which the kettle does not.

### Mechanism

During hard intervals, the heart, lungs and muscles work close to their maximum. Repeated near-maximal efforts stimulate cardiac adaptations (larger stroke volume) and muscle adaptations (more mitochondria, better capillarization) through signals such as AMPK and PGC-1 alpha. Rest periods allow partial recovery, so more time is spent at high intensity than in one continuous effort.

**Common protocols.**

| Protocol | Work | Recovery | Repetitions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Norwegian 4 × 4 | 4 min at 85 to 95 percent HRmax | 3 min active | 4 |
| Tabata | 20 s at about 170 percent VO2 max | 10 s | 7 to 8 |
| Sprint intervals (Wingate) | 30 s all-out | 4 min | 4 to 6 |
| 10 × 1 min | 1 min at about 90 percent HRmax | 1 min | 10 |

See [VO2 max](../../physiology/respiration/vo2-max.md) and [Energy systems](../../physiology/energy-systems.md).

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the claimed effect and how strong the evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Improved VO2 max.** At least as large as moderate continuous training, in less time. Evidence: strong.
- **Benefits in cardiometabolic disease.** A meta-analysis found larger VO2 max gains than moderate training in patients. Evidence: moderate.
- **Greater fat loss than continuous training.** Small or no difference when energy expenditure is similar. Evidence: limited.

## Worked example

A recreational runner replaces one 45-minute easy run per week with a 4 × 4 session: 10 minutes warm-up, four 4-minute intervals at 90 percent of maximal heart rate with 3-minute easy jogs, and 5 minutes cool-down, about 40 minutes in total. Over 8 weeks, VO2 max typically rises more than with the easy run alone.

## Trade offs and when to use it

HIIT is time-efficient and effective, but it is demanding, needs good recovery and is not suitable for everyone without medical clearance (for example people with unstable heart disease).

## Common mistakes

- **Doing HIIT every day.** Two to three sessions per week are usually enough.
- **Intervals that are not hard enough.** Work periods must reach high intensity.
