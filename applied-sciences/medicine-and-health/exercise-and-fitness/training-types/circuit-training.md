# Circuit training

## Summary

Circuit training combines resistance and endurance training: several exercises are done one after another with little rest, forming a circuit that is repeated. It improves muscular endurance, strength and aerobic fitness at the same time, and is efficient for people with limited time. A meta-analysis found that resistance circuit training improves VO2 max and upper-body strength in healthy adults.

## Prerequisites

No prior notes are needed.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Circuit training* is a training format in which a series of exercises (stations) is performed in sequence with short rests, and the whole series is repeated.

### Analogy

Circuit training is like a cooking class where you move between stations every few minutes: chopping, frying, baking. You keep busy the whole time and practice many skills in one session. The analogy breaks because in circuit training fatigue from one station affects the next.

### Mechanism

Moving between exercises for different muscle groups keeps heart rate elevated (endurance stimulus) while each muscle group gets resistance work and rests while the others work. Typical circuits use 6 to 12 stations, 30 to 60 seconds of work or 10 to 20 repetitions per station, 15 to 30 seconds of transition, and 2 to 4 rounds.

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the claimed effect and how strong the evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Improved VO2 max and strength.** Shown in a meta-analysis of resistance circuit training. Evidence: moderate.
- **Maximal strength comparable to traditional training.** Smaller gains for heavy strength than traditional sets with full rest. Evidence: limited.

## Worked example

A person with 30 minutes does 3 rounds of a 6-station circuit: goblet squat, push-up, dumbbell row, reverse lunge, plank and jumping jacks, 40 seconds each with 20 seconds of transition. Each round takes 6 minutes, and the whole session with warm-up fits in 30 minutes.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Circuits are time-efficient and versatile, but they are not ideal for maximal strength or high-skill lifts, because fatigue and short rests limit load.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting similar exercises back to back.** Alternate muscle groups.
- **Using heavy, technical lifts.** Choose exercises that remain safe under fatigue.
