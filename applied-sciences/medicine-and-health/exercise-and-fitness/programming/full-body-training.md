# Full body training (FBW)

## Summary

Full body workout (FBW) training works the whole body in every session, aiming at overall development. All muscle groups are trained each week, from the largest to the smallest, with bilateral and unilateral exercises and pushing and pulling movements for the lower and upper body. FBW suits people with limited time and beginners with three training days a week, and research shows that frequent training of each muscle with moderate volume per session is effective for hypertrophy in trained people too.

- [Training programming](training-programming.md)

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Full body training* is a training split in which each session trains all major muscle groups. It uses *bilateral* (both legs or arms) and *unilateral* (single leg or arm) exercises, and push and pull movements: for the lower body, knee-dominant (push) and hip-dominant (pull) patterns; for the upper body, vertical and horizontal pushes and pulls.

### Analogy

Full body training is like watering a whole garden a little every other day, rather than flooding one bed per week. Every plant gets water often, so none dries out. The analogy breaks because muscles need hard enough sessions (enough water at once) to respond, so very light frequent sessions do not work.

### Mechanism

**Planning an FBW session.**

1. Start with the lower body, including push (squat, lunge) and pull (deadlift, hip thrust) movements.
2. Move to the upper body, using pressing and pulling exercises in the vertical or horizontal plane depending on the goal.
3. Finish with accessory exercises aimed at the trainee's weakest links.
4. On the following days of the microcycle, change the movement patterns and planes.

**Frequency and protein synthesis.** Muscle protein synthesis after a session stays elevated for about 24 to 48 hours, shorter in trained people. Training each muscle more often may therefore keep synthesis elevated more of the week. FBW is sometimes described as good for advanced trainees for this reason. A review (Schoenfeld and colleagues, 2019) found that when weekly volume is equal, frequency has little effect on hypertrophy; higher frequency mainly helps distribute volume. For beginners, FBW works well with 3 training days a week, leaving 48 to 72 hours between sessions for each body part.

### Advantages

- Suits people with little time: three well-planned sessions train all muscle groups, improve well-being and general fitness.
- Volume per muscle per session is moderate, so sessions are less exhausting and recovery is easier.
- Depending on repetitions, load and rest, it can target muscle building or endurance.
- Sessions are varied across the week, which helps people who get bored with routine.

## Worked example

A person with 3 hours per week wants to gain muscle. FBW with 3 sessions of 60 minutes, each with 5 exercises of 3 sets, gives about 9 sets per week per major muscle group, near the volume found effective in meta-analyses. A body part split with the same 3 hours would train each muscle once a week with similar total volume but more fatigue per session.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- FBW limits the volume per muscle per session, which may be a constraint for advanced bodybuilders wanting very high volumes; sessions with many exercises can be long.

## Common mistakes

- **Doing the same exercises every session.** Vary patterns across the week.
- **Putting the most demanding lifts at the end.** Start with large compound lifts.

## Practice

1. What does FBW stand for?
2. How should an FBW session be ordered?
3. When volume is equal, does training frequency change hypertrophy much?

### Answers

1. Full body workout.
2. Lower body push and pull first, then upper body push and pull, then accessories for weak points.
3. Not much; frequency mainly helps distribute volume.
