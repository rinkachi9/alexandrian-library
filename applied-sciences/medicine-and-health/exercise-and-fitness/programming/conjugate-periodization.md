# Conjugate periodization

## Summary

Conjugate periodization changes several training parameters and combines several training methods at the same time within each week. It suits developing strength, endurance and muscle mass together. A typical three-day week starts with a very high-intensity day with few repetitions on bilateral exercises, continues with a hypertrophy day of unilateral exercises, and ends with an endurance day of many repetitions with light loads. The next week keeps the same pattern with heavier loads.

## Prerequisites

- [Periodization](periodization.md)

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Conjugate (concurrent) periodization* trains multiple abilities in parallel within each microcycle, using different methods on different days. The term is associated with the Westside Barbell system of Louie Simmons, which rotates max effort and dynamic effort days, and with Soviet sports science that combined several qualities.

### Analogy

Conjugate periodization is like a balanced weekly menu: protein one day, vegetables another, grains another, so every nutrient appears each week. No food group is dropped for a month. The analogy breaks because training stimuli can interfere with each other more than foods do.

### Mechanism

Training several qualities each week keeps all of them from fading, at the cost of less concentration on any one. Varying exercises (as in Westside's rotation of max effort exercises) reduces staleness.

The diagram shows an example week.

```mermaid

  D1["Day 1: strength, 5 × 5 at RPE 9, bilateral"] --> D2["Day 2: hypertrophy, 4 × 6-12 at RPE 7-8, unilateral"]
  D2 --> D3["Day 3: endurance, 3 × 15-30 at RPE 6"]
  D3 --> NEXT["Next week: same pattern, heavier loads"]
```

## Worked example

A recreational athlete wants strength, size and stamina. Monday: back squat and bench press 5 × 5 at RPE 9. Wednesday: split squats and dumbbell presses 4 × 6 to 12 at RPE 7 to 8. Friday: goblet squats and push-ups 3 × 15 to 30 at RPE 6. Each week, loads are increased slightly while keeping the same structure, so all three qualities progress together.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- Good for people who want several qualities at once and for preventing boredom; less effective than block periodization for peaking a single quality.

## Common mistakes

- **Making every day hard.** Each day has a different emphasis and effort.
- **Rotating exercises so often that progress cannot be measured.**

## Practice

1. What is the main idea of conjugate periodization?
2. Describe a three-day conjugate week.
3. When is conjugate periodization less suitable?

### Answers

1. Training several abilities in parallel within each week with different methods.
2. A high-intensity strength day, a hypertrophy day and an endurance day, with loads rising week to week.
3. When one quality must be peaked for a specific date.
