# Constant tension

## Summary

Constant tension means keeping the muscle loaded throughout the set, avoiding positions where it relaxes. Every moment of relaxation during a set is a moment of rest, even though the exercise is technically continuing. Keeping tension constant gives the trained muscle the maximal stimulus, raises metabolic stress and allows growth with lighter loads.

## Prerequisites

- [Repetition ranges and load](../training-variables/rep-ranges-and-load.md)

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Constant tension* is a technique that avoids lockout or rest positions during a set, so the muscle stays loaded from the first to the last repetition.

### Analogy

Constant tension is like holding a bag of shopping with your arm slightly bent the whole time, instead of resting it on your hip between steps. Your arm tires much faster. The analogy breaks because constant tension also restricts blood flow, which matters for the muscle's response.

### Mechanism

Locking out a squat or pausing at the top of a curl lets the muscle rest briefly. Avoiding these positions keeps the muscle contracting, compresses blood vessels and limits blood flow, which raises metabolite accumulation. A study (Tanimoto and Ishii, 2006) found that low-intensity training with slow movement and constant tension (about 50 percent of 1RM) produced hypertrophy and strength gains similar to normal-speed training at about 80 percent of 1RM.

## Worked example

In leg presses, a lifter stops a few degrees before locking the knees and lowers immediately, for 12 repetitions at a 3-0-1-0 tempo. Using 60 percent of the usual load, the set feels as hard as a heavier set with lockouts.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Constant tension suits hypertrophy with lighter loads and joint-friendly training; it is not for maximal strength work.

## Common mistakes

- **Bouncing out of the bottom.** Control the turnaround.
- **Using the method with very heavy loads.** It works best with moderate loads.
