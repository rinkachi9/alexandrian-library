# Rep range combination

## Summary

In the rep range combination method, each session for a muscle group uses a different repetition range, often with a different exercise. Training legs three times a week might mean squats for 6 repetitions on Monday, leg presses for 12 on Wednesday and leg extensions for 25 on Friday. The method does not specifically develop slow or fast fibers, because heavy and light training affect both fiber types in similar ways, but combining ranges may improve endurance and the muscle's capacity to produce energy aerobically.

## Prerequisites

- [Repetition ranges and load](../training-variables/rep-ranges-and-load.md)

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Rep range combination* is a form of daily undulating loading in which heavy, moderate and light repetition ranges are spread across the sessions of a week for the same muscle group.

### Analogy

The method is like a musician practicing scales slowly, at medium tempo and fast in the same week. Each tempo trains something slightly different, and together they make a more complete player. The analogy breaks because, unlike tempos, rep ranges produce largely similar muscle growth when effort is equal.

### Mechanism

Different rep ranges stress different qualities: heavy loads favor maximal strength and neural adaptation, moderate loads give efficient hypertrophy, and light loads with many repetitions add metabolic stress and local endurance.

**Fiber types.** The idea that low repetitions build fast fibers and high repetitions slow fibers is not supported. Studies comparing heavy and light training taken close to failure show similar growth of type I and type II fibers, with some studies finding a slightly greater type I response to light loads.

**Other effects.** Research suggests combined ranges improve local muscular endurance, mitochondrial capacity to produce energy and muscle oxygenation, mainly through the high-repetition sessions.

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the claimed effect and how strong the evidence in trained or untrained adults is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Similar hypertrophy to a single range.** Total hypertrophy is similar when effort and volume are equal. Evidence: moderate.
- **Selective development of fiber types.** Heavy and light loads affect both fiber types similarly. Evidence: not supported.
- **Better local endurance and mitochondrial function.** Mainly from the high-repetition sessions. Evidence: limited.

## Worked example

A lifter trains chest three times a week: Monday bench press 4 × 6 at about 80 percent of 1RM, Wednesday dumbbell press 3 × 12, Friday cable flies 2 × 25, each set at RIR 1 to 2. Weekly volume is 9 hard sets. Over a block they track the top set in each range and progress each separately.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Combining ranges gives variety, trains strength and endurance together and spreads joint stress. It is less suited to peaking maximal strength, where heavy work dominates.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing light sets build only slow fibers.** Both fiber types grow with light and heavy loads near failure.
- **Stopping high-repetition sets far from failure.** Light sets need high effort to stimulate growth.

## Practice

1. Give an example of rep range combination for legs.
2. Does this method train fiber types selectively?
3. Which effects may come from combining ranges?

### Answers

1. Squat 6 reps, leg press 12 reps, leg extension 25 reps on three days.
2. No; heavy and light loads affect both fiber types similarly.
3. Better local endurance, mitochondrial capacity and muscle oxygenation.
