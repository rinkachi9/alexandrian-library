# Myo-reps

## Summary

Myo-reps is a rest-pause technique for hypertrophy: an activation set close to failure, followed by several short "myo sets" separated by 10 to 20 second rests, all with the same load. The aim is to keep high-threshold motor units under high mechanical tension for many repetitions in little time. The method was developed by Borge Fagerli and resembles rest-pause training, which research shows can produce similar or slightly greater hypertrophy than traditional sets in less time.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Myo-reps* is a rest-pause method in which an activation set near failure is followed by mini-sets with very short rests. The name refers to myofilaments, the contractile proteins whose function declines with fatigue.

### Analogy

Myo-reps is like making several short sprints after one long run, with only a few breaths between them. The first run gets you tired; each short sprint keeps you working at a high level for longer than a fresh start would allow. The analogy breaks because the "breaths" in myo-reps are deliberately too short for full recovery.

### Mechanism

The method aims for high stimulation of muscle fibers by creating fatigue in short intervals. This leads to a shortage of energy substrates, accumulation of metabolites and reduced function of the myofilaments. The fundamental idea is practical: once the activation set has recruited high-threshold motor units, every repetition after a short rest is an "effective" repetition with those units under high tension.

**Protocol.**

1. Choose a load of about 8 to 20RM (about 50 to 80 percent of 1RM).
2. Do the activation set at RPE 8 to 10 (no more than 2 repetitions in reserve). This set recruits the high-threshold motor units that produce the most force.
3. Rest for no more than 10 to 20 seconds.
4. Do the first myo set with the same load at RPE 9 to 10 (no more than 1 repetition in reserve).
5. The first myo set should not drop below 5 to 8 repetitions, because the goal is enough volume for hypertrophy. If repetitions fall below this, either accept lower volume or reduce the load or use an easier variation.
6. Rest 10 to 20 seconds.
7. Do the second myo set with the same load (or apply rule 5 if volume was too low), at RPE 9 to 10.
8. Rest 10 to 20 seconds.
9. Do the third myo set at RPE 10, a maximal effort.

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the claimed effect and how strong the evidence in trained or untrained adults is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Similar hypertrophy in less time.** Rest-pause training produced similar or slightly better results than traditional sets in some trials. Evidence: limited.
- **High-threshold motor units are recruited in every myo repetition.** Consistent with the size principle, but direct evidence for the protocol is limited. Evidence: limited.

## Worked example

Myo-reps fit calisthenics as well, for example push-ups, with a weighted vest for advanced trainees.

Suppose your maximum is 15 push-ups (15RM, RPE 10). The activation set at RPE 8 to 10 means 13 to 15 repetitions; it is a good day and you match your record of 15. You rest 10 to 20 seconds. In the first myo set you get 11 repetitions. Another 10 to 20 seconds of rest, and the second myo set gives 9. After the next rest, the third myo set reaches only 7.

Your goal was 50 push-ups in total, and you have 42 (15 + 11 + 9 + 7). The next myo set would give about 5 repetitions, and here you hit the wall described in rule 5. You can either lower the volume and do one last maximal set of 3 push-ups (RPE 10), finishing at 45, or keep the higher volume by taking off the vest or using an easier variation (for example push-ups on a low step) and doing a maximal set of 6, finishing at 48.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Myo-reps save time and suit isolation exercises, machines and bodyweight movements. They are less suitable for heavy free-weight compound lifts, because repeated sets near failure with short rests break technique.

## Common mistakes

- **Resting too long between myo sets.** Longer rests make it a series of ordinary sets.
- **Using myo-reps on heavy squats or deadlifts.** Choose safe exercises for repeated sets near failure.

## Practice

1. What is the load range for myo-reps?
2. How long are the rests between myo sets?
3. What should you do when a myo set drops below the minimum repetitions?

### Answers

1. About 8 to 20RM, roughly 50 to 80 percent of 1RM.
2. 10 to 20 seconds at most.
3. Accept lower volume with one last maximal set, or reduce the load or use an easier variation.
