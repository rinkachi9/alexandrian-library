# Drop set

## Summary

A drop set is a set taken to or near failure, followed immediately by a load reduction of about 30 percent and another set to failure without rest. It is one of the most popular ways to add volume to an exercise in little time. A 2023 meta-analysis found that drop sets give similar hypertrophy to traditional sets with equal volume, in less time.

## Core concepts

### Definition

A *drop set* is a sequence of two or more sets of the same exercise with decreasing loads and no rest between them, each continued to near failure.

### Analogy

A drop set is like running until you cannot keep your pace, then continuing at a jog, then at a walk. You keep moving after the first "failure" by lowering the demand. The analogy breaks because the muscle, unlike the runner, recovers within seconds partly, so each drop brings a few more repetitions.

### Mechanism

Failure at a given load means the remaining motor units cannot produce that force. Lowering the load lets the same fatigued, high-threshold motor units keep working for more repetitions, which adds mechanical and metabolic stress without the time cost of full rest periods.

Typical structure: a planned number of repetitions or a set to near failure, then a drop of about 20 to 30 percent of the load and maximal repetitions, repeated once or twice.

### Claimed effects and evidence

Each entry states the claimed effect and how strong the evidence in trained or untrained adults is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../../nutrition/evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Similar hypertrophy with less time.** A meta-analysis found no difference in hypertrophy versus traditional sets when volume was equal. Evidence: moderate.
- **Greater hypertrophy than traditional sets.** Not shown when volume is equated. Evidence: not supported.

## Worked example

On cable lateral raises, a lifter does 12 repetitions with 10 kg, drops immediately to 7 kg for 8 more repetitions and to 5 kg for 6 more. The whole sequence takes about 90 seconds, compared with about 6 minutes for three straight sets with rest.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Drop sets save time and add volume, but they are fatiguing and less suitable for heavy compound lifts where failure is risky. They work best on machines, cables and dumbbells where loads can change quickly.

## Common mistakes

- **Resting between drops.** The method relies on no rest; change loads quickly.
- **Using drop sets on every exercise.** Fatigue accumulates; use them on one or two exercises.

## Practice

1. How much is the load usually reduced in a drop set?
2. What did the 2023 meta-analysis find?
3. Which exercises suit drop sets best?

### Answers

1. About 20 to 30 percent.
2. Similar hypertrophy to traditional sets at equal volume, in less time.
3. Machines, cables and dumbbells where loads change quickly.
