# Vitamin B1 (thiamin)

## Summary

Thiamin (vitamin B1) is a water-soluble B vitamin. As thiamin pyrophosphate (TPP), it is a coenzyme for key steps in carbohydrate metabolism: the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA and a step of the Krebs cycle. Deficiency causes beriberi (heart failure and neuropathy) and Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome, mainly in people with alcohol use disorder.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Thiamin* is a water-soluble vitamin of the B group. Its active form, *thiamin pyrophosphate* (TPP, also called thiamin diphosphate), is a coenzyme for pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, branched-chain keto acid dehydrogenase and transketolase.

### Analogy

Thiamin is like a ticket inspector at the entrance of the Krebs cycle: pyruvate from glucose can enter only with its help. Without the inspector, pyruvate piles up as lactate, and the brain and heart, which depend on glucose, run out of energy. The analogy breaks because thiamin also helps at other steps inside the cycle.

### Mechanism

TPP enables decarboxylation of alpha-keto acids:

- pyruvate to acetyl-CoA (pyruvate dehydrogenase), linking glycolysis to the Krebs cycle ([Krebs cycle](../../../../natural-sciences/chemistry/biochemistry/krebs-cycle.md));
- alpha-ketoglutarate to succinyl-CoA within the Krebs cycle;
- breakdown of branched-chain amino acids;
- transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway.

Deficiency reduces ATP production in tissues with high glucose use (brain, nerves, heart). Body stores last only about 2 to 3 weeks. Alcohol reduces thiamin absorption and storage.

### Proposed effects and evidence

Each entry states the proposed effect and how strong the human evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Energy metabolism.** Essential coenzyme for converting carbohydrates to energy. Evidence: established physiology.
- **Nervous system function.** Deficiency causes neuropathy (dry beriberi): numbness, tingling and poor coordination. Evidence: established physiology.
- **Muscle function.** Deficiency causes muscle weakness. Evidence: established physiology.
- **Cognitive function.** Deficiency causes memory problems, confusion, Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome. Evidence: established physiology.
- **Protection of cell membranes.** Proposed role; little human evidence. Evidence: very limited.
- **Heart health.** Deficiency causes cardiomyopathy (wet beriberi); supplements may help heart failure patients on diuretics, which deplete thiamin. Evidence: limited.
- **Appetite regulation.** Loss of appetite is an early sign of deficiency. Evidence: limited.
- **Digestive health.** Deficiency affects the gut; benefit of extra thiamin not shown. Evidence: very limited.
- **Protection against metabolic diseases such as diabetes.** Low thiamin is common in diabetes; benefit of supplements unclear. Evidence: limited.
- **Treatment of neurological diseases linked to deficiency.** Thiamin is the standard treatment of Wernicke encephalopathy. Evidence: strong.

## Worked example

A person with long-term heavy alcohol use is admitted with confusion, unsteady walking and abnormal eye movements, the classic signs of Wernicke encephalopathy. Doctors give intravenous thiamin before glucose, because glucose without thiamin can worsen the condition. Early treatment can reverse symptoms; delayed treatment risks permanent Korsakoff syndrome.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Deficiency is rare in people with a varied diet but common with alcohol use disorder, bariatric surgery, prolonged vomiting and some diuretics.

## Common mistakes

- **Giving glucose before thiamin in suspected deficiency.** Give thiamin first.
- **Ignoring thiamin status in alcohol use disorder.** Supplementation is standard.

## Practice

1. What is the active form of thiamin?
2. Which reaction links glycolysis to the Krebs cycle and needs thiamin?
3. Name two diseases of thiamin deficiency.

### Answers

1. Thiamin pyrophosphate.
2. Pyruvate to acetyl-CoA by pyruvate dehydrogenase.
3. Beriberi and Wernicke encephalopathy (Korsakoff syndrome).
