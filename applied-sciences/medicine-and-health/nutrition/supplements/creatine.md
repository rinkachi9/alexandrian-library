# Creatine

## Summary

Creatine is a compound made from the amino acids arginine, glycine and methionine and stored in muscle, mostly as phosphocreatine. Phosphocreatine regenerates ATP during short, intense efforts. Creatine monohydrate (usually 3 to 5 g per day) is the best-studied sports supplement: it increases muscle creatine by about 20 to 40 percent and improves strength, power and lean mass gains when combined with resistance training. It is safe in healthy people. It is not an amino acid or a BCAA, although it is often grouped with them.

*This note is educational. It is not medical advice and does not replace a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist, especially during pregnancy, illness or when taking medicines.*

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Creatine* (methylguanidinoacetic acid) is a nitrogen-containing compound synthesized in the kidneys and liver (about 1 g per day) and obtained from meat and fish (about 1 g per day in omnivores). About 95 percent is stored in skeletal muscle, about two thirds as *phosphocreatine*.

### Analogy

Phosphocreatine is like a small battery pack next to the muscle's main power supply. When a sprint or heavy lift drains ATP in seconds, the battery recharges ATP instantly. Supplementing creatine makes the battery pack bigger, so a few more repetitions or a slightly faster sprint are possible. The analogy breaks because the battery only helps for efforts of up to about 10 seconds.

### Mechanism

Creatine kinase transfers a phosphate from phosphocreatine to ADP, regenerating ATP within milliseconds ([Energy systems](../../physiology/energy-systems.md), [ATP](../../../../natural-sciences/chemistry/biochemistry/atp.md)). This system dominates the first seconds of maximal effort. More stored phosphocreatine allows slightly more work in repeated high-intensity efforts, which over weeks leads to more training volume and greater gains. Creatine also draws water into muscle cells (cell volumization), adding 1 to 2 kg of body weight in the first weeks, and may have direct effects on muscle signaling (including mRNA expression of growth-related genes) and on the brain.

**Dosing.** Either a loading phase (about 20 g per day in four doses for 5 to 7 days, then 3 to 5 g per day) or 3 to 5 g daily from the start; both reach the same muscle levels, the second within about 3 to 4 weeks.

### Proposed effects and evidence

Each entry states the proposed effect and how strong the human evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Greater strength and power with resistance training.** Consistent in meta-analyses. Evidence: strong.
- **Greater lean mass gains.** About 1 to 2 kg more over training programs, partly water. Evidence: strong.
- **Improved repeated sprint performance.** Consistent effect in short, repeated efforts. Evidence: strong.
- **Faster recovery and less muscle damage.** Some evidence; inconsistent. Evidence: limited.
- **Cognitive benefits, especially under sleep deprivation or in vegetarians.** Small trials show benefit. Evidence: limited.
- **Kidney damage in healthy people.** Not found in long-term studies; creatinine in blood rises, which can be misread as kidney problems. Evidence: not supported.
- **Is a branched-chain amino acid.** Creatine is not an amino acid; the BCAAs are leucine, isoleucine and valine. Evidence: not supported.

## Worked example

A lifter takes 5 g creatine monohydrate daily with breakfast. After 4 weeks, body weight is up 1.5 kg and they get one or two more repetitions on bench press sets with the same load. Over 12 weeks, they gain about 1 kg more lean mass than they would without creatine. Their routine blood test shows creatinine slightly above normal; their doctor confirms this is expected with creatine and kidney function is normal.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Creatine monohydrate is cheap, effective and safe; more expensive forms (ethyl ester, buffered) are not better. People with kidney disease should consult a doctor. Some people are non-responders, usually those with high baseline muscle creatine (high meat intake).

## Common mistakes

- **Calling creatine an amino acid or BCAA.** It is a separate compound made from three amino acids.
- **Stopping creatine because of early weight gain.** The gain is mostly water in muscle.
- **Paying for special forms.** Monohydrate is the reference form.

## Practice

1. How does creatine improve short, intense efforts?
2. What daily dose is typical?
3. Why does blood creatinine rise with creatine?

### Answers

1. Phosphocreatine regenerates ATP quickly.
2. 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate.
3. Creatine is converted to creatinine, so the marker rises without kidney damage.
