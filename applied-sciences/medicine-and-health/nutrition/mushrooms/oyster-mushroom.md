# Oyster mushroom

## Summary

The oyster mushroom (Pleurotus ostreatus) is a common, inexpensive cultivated mushroom and one of the richest dietary sources of ergothioneine, an amino acid derivative sometimes called a "longevity vitamin". Humans cannot make ergothioneine but have a specific transporter that concentrates it in tissues exposed to oxidative stress. Low blood levels are associated with older age, frailty and cognitive decline. Whether eating more improves health is not yet shown.

*This note is educational. It is not medical advice and does not replace a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist, especially during pregnancy, illness or when taking medicines.*

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Ergothioneine* is a sulfur-containing derivative of histidine made only by fungi and some bacteria. It is taken up by the transporter OCTN1 (SLC22A4). *Pleurotus ostreatus* is the common oyster mushroom; oyster and king oyster mushrooms contain several milligrams of ergothioneine per 100 g.

### Analogy

Ergothioneine is like a specialized protective coating the body cannot make but carefully collects: it has a dedicated delivery system (OCTN1) and keeps it in places of high wear. The analogy breaks because proof that more coating means better health is still missing.

### Mechanism

Ergothioneine is a stable antioxidant and metal chelator. It accumulates in red blood cells, liver, kidney, eye lens and bone marrow through OCTN1. Bruce Ames proposed it as a "longevity vitamin", a hypothesis rather than an established classification.

### Proposed effects and evidence

Each entry states the proposed effect and how strong the human evidence is, using the grades defined in the [Evidence grades](../evidence-grades.md) note.

- **Rich source of ergothioneine.** Oyster mushrooms have among the highest food levels. Evidence: established physiology.
- **Protection against age-related disease.** Low blood levels associated with frailty and cognitive decline; no intervention trials with outcomes. Evidence: very limited.

## Worked example

Cooking 100 g of oyster mushrooms in a sauce provides several milligrams of ergothioneine, much more than most foods. They are also a source of fiber (beta-glucans), protein and B vitamins.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Oyster mushrooms are a safe, inexpensive food; they are a good choice for those interested in ergothioneine without supplements.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling ergothioneine an established vitamin.** The "longevity vitamin" idea is a hypothesis.

## Practice

1. What is ergothioneine?
2. Which transporter concentrates ergothioneine?
3. Is it proven that ergothioneine extends life?

### Answers

1. A sulfur-containing histidine derivative made by fungi.
2. OCTN1.
3. No.
