# Military sleep method

## Summary

The "military sleep method" is a relaxation routine for falling asleep quickly: slow breathing, relaxing the face, dropping the shoulders and arms, relaxing the body from top to bottom, and clearing the mind with a calm image or by repeating "don't think". It was described in a 1981 book by the coach Bud Winter, who said it helped naval pilots fall asleep in two minutes after six weeks of practice. Its parts resemble well-studied relaxation techniques, but the method itself has not been tested in controlled trials.

## Core concepts

### Definition

The *military sleep method* is a sequence of body relaxation and mental imagery steps performed in bed to reduce physical tension and arousal before sleep.

### Analogy

The method is like shutting down a building floor by floor: switch off the lights on the top floor (face), then the next floors (shoulders, arms, chest, legs), and finally turn off the alarm system (racing thoughts). When every floor is dark, the building is ready for the night. The analogy breaks because thoughts are not switched off by a single button; they need redirection.

### Mechanism

The steps combine progressive relaxation, slow breathing and cognitive distraction:

1. **Breathe deeply.** Close the eyes and take slow, deep breaths.
2. **Relax the face.** Relax all the facial muscles, starting at the forehead and moving down over the cheeks, mouth and jaw, one part at a time, including the tongue and the muscles around the eyes.
3. **Drop the shoulders and arms.** Relax the neck, let the shoulders drop and sink into the bed. Relax one arm from the biceps to the forearm, hand and fingers, then the other.
4. **Work downward.** Relax the chest, abdomen and pelvis, then one leg from the thigh to the knee, calf, ankle, foot and toes, then the other leg.
5. **Clear the mind.** Focus on a calming image, such as lying in a meadow under a blue sky or in a velvet hammock in a dark room. If imagery does not work, repeat "don't think" for about 10 seconds; when other thoughts intrude, set them aside and return to the image or phrase.

Related activities with research support:

- **Deep, slow breathing** relaxes the body, calms the nervous system and slows the heart (Zaccaro and colleagues, 2018). Claims that it triggers melatonin production are not established.
- **Progressive muscle relaxation**, contracting and releasing muscles one by one, bottom-up or top-down (see [Progressive muscle relaxation (Jacobson)](../../physiotherapy/progressive-muscle-relaxation.md)).
- **Biofeedback**, using devices to learn to control breathing, heart rate, muscle tension and other functions.
- **Visualization**, focusing on calming images to produce relaxation.

## Worked example

A student who usually lies awake for 40 minutes practices the routine nightly. The first nights, it takes about 10 minutes to complete and sleep still takes 30 minutes. After two weeks, the steps become automatic, and the student falls asleep in about 15 minutes. This individual result fits with what relaxation training achieves in trials, but it is not proof that this specific method works better than other relaxation methods.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- Free, safe and easy to learn; the claim of falling asleep in two minutes comes from an anecdotal source.
- For chronic insomnia, cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia, which includes relaxation among other parts, is the evidence-based treatment.

## Common mistakes

- **Trying hard to fall asleep.** Effort increases arousal; the method works best done passively.
- **Expecting results on the first night.** The original description assumes several weeks of practice.

## Practice

1. List the five steps of the military sleep method.
2. What is the origin of the method?
3. Which well-studied techniques does it resemble?

### Answers

1. Slow breathing, relaxing the face, dropping the shoulders and arms, relaxing the body downward, clearing the mind with an image or phrase.
2. A 1981 book by the coach Bud Winter, describing training of naval pilots.
3. Progressive muscle relaxation, slow breathing and visualization.
