# Wrist joint

## Summary

The wrist (radiocarpal) joint is an ellipsoid joint between the lower end of the radius and the proximal row of carpal bones. It allows flexion, extension and side-to-side movement (radial and ulnar deviation), but not rotation, which comes from the forearm.

- [Joints overview](joints-overview.md)

## Core concepts

### Definition

The *radiocarpal joint* is a biaxial ellipsoid synovial joint between the radius (and the triangular fibrocartilage over the ulna) and the scaphoid, lunate and triquetrum. The midcarpal joint between the two carpal rows contributes to wrist movement. Latin: *articulatio radiocarpalis*.

### Analogy

The wrist is like an egg sitting in a spoon: the egg (convex carpal row) can tip forward, back and side to side in the spoon (concave radius), but cannot spin. The analogy breaks because the "egg" is made of several bones that shift relative to each other.

### Mechanism

Flexion is about 80 degrees and extension about 70 degrees; ulnar deviation (about 30 to 40 degrees) is greater than radial deviation (about 20 degrees), because the radial styloid blocks the radial side. The forearm flexors and extensors cross the wrist and move it.

### Facts

| Feature | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Synovial, biaxial, ellipsoid |
| Bones | Radius, scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum |
| Movements | Flexion, extension, radial and ulnar deviation, circumduction |
| Rotation | Comes from the radioulnar joints, not the wrist |

## Worked example

Carpal tunnel syndrome occurs when the median nerve is compressed under the flexor retinaculum on the palm side of the wrist. Tingling in the thumb, index and middle fingers, often worse at night, is typical. Wrist splints at night help many people; persistent cases may need surgical release.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Two axes give dexterity, and leaving rotation to the forearm keeps the wrist stable.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying the wrist rotates.** Pronation and supination happen in the forearm.

## Practice

1. What type of joint is the wrist?
2. Why is ulnar deviation greater than radial deviation?

### Answers

1. An ellipsoid (condylar) synovial joint.
2. The radial styloid process limits movement to the radial side.
