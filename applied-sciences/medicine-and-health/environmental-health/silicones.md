# Silicones

## Summary

Silicones is used in cosmetics and hair products (dimethicone, cyclopentasiloxane). The concern raised in the notes: silicones may make skin and hair less able to "breathe", drying them out and clogging pores. Skin does not breathe in the sense implied; dimethicone forms a breathable, non-occlusive film and is used in barrier creams. It is non-comedogenic in tests. Silicones can build up on hair and make it feel heavy, which clarifying shampoo removes. The real concern is environmental: cyclic siloxanes D4 and D5 persist in the environment and are restricted in EU wash-off cosmetics since 2020.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Silicones* are polymers with a silicon-oxygen backbone. *Dimethicone* is a linear silicone; *cyclopentasiloxane* (D5) is a volatile cyclic silicone.

### Analogy

Dimethicone on hair is like a thin raincoat that keeps hair smooth and protects it from heat; it does not seal it airtight. The analogy breaks because, unlike a raincoat, it can build up with repeated use.

### Mechanism

Silicones are hydrophobic, smooth and inert. On skin, they reduce water loss without blocking gas exchange. On hair, they reduce friction and frizz. Volatile cyclic siloxanes evaporate; D4 is toxic to aquatic organisms and persistent.

### Claimed risks and evidence

Each entry states the claimed risk and how strong the evidence for it is at typical exposures, using the grades in [Evidence grades](../nutrition/evidence-grades.md).

- **Skin "cannot breathe" and gets dry.** Not supported; dimethicone is used in barrier creams. Evidence: not supported.
- **Clogged pores.** Dimethicone is non-comedogenic in tests. Evidence: not supported.
- **Build-up on hair.** Can occur with heavy silicones; removed by clarifying shampoo. Evidence: limited.
- **Environmental persistence of D4 and D5.** Documented; EU restricted them in wash-off products. Evidence: strong.

## Worked example

A person with fine hair finds conditioners make it limp. Switching to a lighter conditioner or using clarifying shampoo weekly solves the build-up; there is no health reason to avoid silicones.

## Trade offs and when to use it

Silicones are safe for skin and hair; environmental concerns relate to cyclic siloxanes washed into water.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking silicones suffocate the skin.** Skin gets oxygen from blood, and dimethicone is breathable.

## Practice

1. What is dimethicone?
2. Why were D4 and D5 restricted in the EU?
3. Does dimethicone clog pores?

### Answers

1. A linear silicone used in cosmetics.
2. Environmental persistence and toxicity to aquatic life.
3. No, it is non-comedogenic.
