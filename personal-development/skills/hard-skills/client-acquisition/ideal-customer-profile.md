# Ideal customer profile

## Summary

An ideal customer profile (ICP) describes the kind of organization or person that gets the most value from what you sell and brings you the most value in return: they need it, can pay for it, are easy to reach and to serve, and stay. Buyer personas go one level deeper and describe the individuals involved in the decision. Both are built from evidence, mainly an analysis of your best and worst existing clients, interviews and market data, not from imagination. A clear ICP makes every later step of acquisition more effective, because messages, channels and offers can be specific, and it gives a reason to decline clients who would cost more than they bring.

[Client acquisition fundamentals](client-acquisition-fundamentals.md). [Customer research](../copywriting/customer-research.md) describes research methods that also apply here.

## Core concepts

### Definitions

- An *ideal customer profile* (ICP) is a description of the type of account (company or individual client) that is the best fit for your offer, defined by observable characteristics such as industry, size, location, technology, situation and needs.
- A *buyer persona* is a semi-fictional description of a person involved in buying, based on research: role, goals, problems, decision criteria, information sources and objections.
- *Segmentation* is dividing a market into groups with similar needs or characteristics. The ICP picks the most attractive segment.

*Analogy.* An ICP is like a fishing guide's knowledge of where a particular fish lives. A beginner throws bait anywhere and catches a few random fish. The guide knows the depth, temperature, time of day and bait that the target fish prefers, and goes there with the right gear. The analogy breaks because fish do not talk back, while ideal clients can tell you exactly why they chose you, which is the most valuable input for the profile.

### Why it matters

- **Message relevance:** a message written for a specific situation is more persuasive than one written for everyone (see [value proposition and benefits](../copywriting/value-proposition-and-benefits.md)).
- **Channel choice:** knowing who they are tells you where to reach them.
- **Efficiency:** time is spent on prospects likely to buy.
- **Better clients:** good-fit clients are easier to serve, more satisfied and more likely to refer others.

### Building an ICP from your clients

1. **List clients** from the last one to three years.
2. **Score each** on value (revenue, margin, length of relationship), fit (results achieved, ease of working together) and advocacy (referrals, testimonials).
3. **Identify the best 20%** and the worst 20%.
4. **Compare characteristics:** industry, size, location, growth stage, technology used, the trigger that made them look for help, who bought, how they found you.
5. **Interview** several of the best clients: why they chose you, what problem they had, what alternatives they considered, what result they value most.
6. **Write the profile** and the disqualifiers (characteristics of poor-fit clients).

New businesses without clients start with a hypothesis from market research and interviews, then revise it after the first clients.

### ICP template (B2B)

| Dimension | Example: bookkeeping service for creatives |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Sole traders and small studios (1 to 10 people) |
| Industry | Design, illustration, photography, video |
| Location | Poland, invoicing domestic and EU clients |
| Situation | Revenue 100,000 to 1 million PLN a year, growing, first employee or VAT registration coming |
| Trigger | A missed deadline, a tax inspection letter, a first large foreign client |
| Pain | Fear of mistakes, time lost on paperwork, accountants who do not understand creative work |
| Buying process | Owner decides alone, often after a recommendation |
| Disqualifiers | Companies needing full payroll for many employees, clients who want the lowest price only |

### Buyer personas

In B2B, a purchase often involves a *buying group*: the person who feels the problem, the decision maker, the budget holder, technical evaluators and sometimes procurement or legal. Gartner has described buying groups of typically six to ten people for complex B2B solutions. Each role has different questions:

| Role | Main concern |
| --- | --- |
| User | Will it make my work easier? |
| Manager or champion | Will it solve the team's problem and make me look good? |
| Budget holder | Is it worth the money? What is the risk? |
| Technical evaluator | Does it fit our systems and security requirements? |
| Procurement or legal | Are the terms acceptable? |

A useful persona contains evidence-based details: goals, problems, how success is measured in their job, information sources, objections, and quotes from interviews.

### Jobs and triggers

Two ideas from customer research sharpen the ICP:

- **Jobs to be done:** what progress the client is trying to make (see [customer research](../copywriting/customer-research.md#jobs-to-be-done)).
- **Trigger events:** changes that create the need: new funding, a new manager, expansion abroad, a regulation, a failed project, rapid growth. Contacting organizations soon after a trigger raises relevance.

### Negative profile

A *negative* or *anti-ICP* lists characteristics of clients you should avoid: unrealistic budgets, constant scope changes, poor payment history, values conflicts, needs outside your expertise. Declining such clients is part of the strategy.

## Worked example

A small web development agency analyzes its 24 clients from three years (invented numbers).

**Scoring** on revenue, margin, satisfaction and referrals shows the best 5 clients:

- all are e-commerce companies selling physical products,
- revenue 5 to 50 million PLN,
- in-house marketing team, no in-house developers,
- came through referrals or the agency's case studies,
- triggered by a platform migration or a failing checkout.

**The worst 5:**

- two startups without funding who changed scope repeatedly,
- one large corporation with a nine month procurement process for a small project,
- two clients who chose on price and disputed invoices.

**New ICP:** e-commerce companies, 5 to 50 million PLN revenue, marketing team without developers, facing a migration or conversion problem. **Disqualifiers:** no defined budget, procurement cycles over three months for projects under 100,000 PLN.

**Consequences:** the agency rewrites its website for this ICP, publishes two migration case studies and builds an outreach list of shops on platforms that are being discontinued.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Narrow ICP** gives focus and relevance and may feel risky. Most small firms find that a niche brings more, not fewer, clients.
- **Data versus intuition.** Few clients give weak data. Combine scoring with interviews.
- **Static versus evolving.** Review the ICP yearly or after major changes.
- **Personas can become stereotypes.** Base them on research and update them.

## Common mistakes

- **ICP based on hopes.** Fix: base it on your best actual clients.
- **Too broad ("small and medium businesses").** Fix: add industry, situation and trigger.
- **Ignoring the buying group.** Fix: describe each role in B2B sales.
- **No disqualifiers.** Fix: write them down and use them.
- **Never revisiting.** Fix: review yearly.

## Practice

1. Distinguish an ICP from a buyer persona.
2. Score five clients or projects you know on value, fit and advocacy, and describe what the best ones share.
3. List three trigger events for a service you know.
4. Write a negative profile of clients you should decline.
