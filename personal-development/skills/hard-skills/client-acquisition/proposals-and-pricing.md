# Proposals and pricing

## Summary

A proposal turns what you learned in discovery into a written offer: the client's situation and goals in their words, the approach, the deliverables, the timeline, the price and the terms. Pricing is the hardest part for most freelancers and small firms. The three main approaches are cost-plus, market-based and value-based pricing, and the strongest position is a price anchored in the value of the result to the client, presented as a few clear options. A proposal is a decision document for the buyer, so clarity and specificity matter more than length, and it should never be the first time the client hears the price.

## Prerequisites

[Sales conversations](sales-conversations.md) for discovery, and [value proposition and benefits](../copywriting/value-proposition-and-benefits.md).

## Core concepts

### Definitions

- A *proposal* is a written offer to provide specified work or products on stated terms. Accepted, it often becomes part of the contract.
- A *statement of work* (SOW) defines deliverables, responsibilities, timeline and acceptance criteria in detail.
- *Scope* is what is included. *Scope creep* is the gradual expansion of work beyond the agreed scope without matching changes in price or time.

*Analogy.* A proposal is like an architect's plan presented to a family who wants to build a house. It restates what the family said they need (three bedrooms, a quiet office), shows how the design meets it, lists what is and is not included, gives the cost and schedule, and offers a few versions at different budgets. A family that sees its own wishes in the plan trusts the architect. The analogy breaks because a house is visible when finished, while many services (strategy, software, consulting) are intangible, so the proposal has to make the outcome concrete in words and numbers.

### Structure of a proposal

1. **Situation and goals:** the client's problem and desired outcome, in their words, with the numbers from discovery.
2. **Approach:** how you will achieve the outcome, in phases.
3. **Deliverables and scope:** what is included, and explicitly what is not.
4. **Timeline:** milestones and dependencies on the client.
5. **Investment:** price, options, payment terms.
6. **Proof:** relevant case studies, testimonials.
7. **Terms:** acceptance, changes, intellectual property, cancellation, confidentiality.
8. **Next step:** how to accept and what happens then.

Keep it as short as the decision allows. Executives often read the first page and the price.

### Pricing approaches

| Approach | How the price is set | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost-plus | Your costs (time × rate, materials) plus margin | Simple, covers costs | Ignores value to the client. Penalizes efficiency |
| Market-based | What competitors charge | Easy to justify | Invites price comparison, no differentiation |
| Value-based | A share of the value the client gets | Aligns price with outcome, can be much higher | Requires understanding the value and a credible estimate |

*Value-based pricing* starts from the economic value of the result to the client: revenue gained, costs saved, risk reduced. If a project is expected to recover 40,000 PLN a month in lost sales, a price of 30,000 is easy to justify, while the same work priced at hours × rate might be 12,000. Value is estimated in discovery (see the implication questions in [sales conversations](sales-conversations.md#spin-questions)).

### Pricing models for services

- **Hourly or daily rate:** flexible, transparent, and caps your income at your hours. Clients bear the risk of overruns.
- **Fixed project price:** the client knows the cost, you bear the risk of overruns and benefit from efficiency.
- **Retainer:** a monthly fee for ongoing work or availability.
- **Performance-based:** a share of results (commission, revenue share). Attractive when results are measurable and you can influence them, risky otherwise.

### Options and anchoring

Offering two or three options (for example basic, standard, extended) gives the client a choice of how to work with you rather than whether to work with you. Showing the extended option first can make the standard option look reasonable, an application of anchoring (see [persuasion principles](../copywriting/persuasion-principles.md#anchoring)). The options must be genuinely different in value, not artificial decoys.

### Negotiation

Clients may negotiate. Principles from negotiation research and practice (see [salary and offer negotiation](../../../career/job-search/salary-and-offer-negotiation.md)):

- **Trade, do not cave:** if the price goes down, the scope, timeline or terms change too.
- **Know your walk-away point** (your BATNA, best alternative to a negotiated agreement, Fisher and Ury).
- **Discuss value, not cost.**
- **Discounts set expectations.** A first project at a deep discount anchors future prices.

### Avoiding scope creep

- Define deliverables and acceptance criteria.
- List exclusions.
- Agree a change process: requests outside scope are quoted separately.
- Keep a written record of decisions.

### Payment terms

Common terms for small service firms: a deposit before work starts (for example 30% to 50%), milestone payments and the balance on delivery, with a payment deadline. In the EU, the Late Payment Directive (2011/7/EU) sets a default payment period of 30 days for B2B transactions unless otherwise agreed, generally capped at 60 days, and gives a right to statutory interest and compensation for recovery costs on late payments. National laws implement it.

## Worked example

A proposal outline for the shop from the discovery call in [sales conversations](sales-conversations.md#worked-example) (invented figures).

**1. Situation.** Since moving to the new platform in March, sales fell about 20%, mostly mobile orders, about 40,000 PLN a month. Goal: restore mobile checkout performance before September.

**2. Approach.** Phase 1: two-week audit of the mobile checkout (analytics, session recordings, testing on 10 devices). Phase 2: fixes prioritized by impact. Phase 3: four weeks of monitoring.

**3. Scope.** Included: checkout and cart pages, mobile and desktop. Not included: product pages, new features, platform migration.

**4. Investment.**

| Option | Includes | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audit only | Phase 1 report with prioritized recommendations | 7,500 PLN |
| Audit and fixes (recommended) | Phases 1 and 2 | 26,000 PLN |
| Audit, fixes and monitoring | Phases 1 to 3, with a follow-up report | 32,000 PLN |

Payment: 40% at signing, 60% on completion of phase 2. Monitoring billed at the end of phase 3.

**5. Proof.** Two case studies of checkout fixes after migrations, with results.

**6. Next step.** Sign by 15 June to start on 20 June.

The recommended option is under the client's stated budget of 30,000 and small relative to the monthly loss.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Value pricing** captures more value and needs trust and a credible value estimate. It is hard with clients who buy on price or for work whose value cannot be estimated.
- **Fixed prices** reward your efficiency and expose you to overruns. Use clear scope and a change process.
- **Detailed versus short proposals.** Long proposals look thorough and are skimmed. Put the decision information first.
- **Discounting** wins a deal and lowers future prices. Trade scope instead.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending a proposal without discovery.** Fix: discover first, propose second.
- **Surprising the client with the price.** Fix: discuss a range in the call.
- **No exclusions.** Fix: write what is not included.
- **Pricing by hours for high-value outcomes.** Fix: consider value-based options.
- **Lowering price without changing scope.** Fix: trade.
