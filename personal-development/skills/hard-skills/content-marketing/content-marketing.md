# Content marketing

## Summary

Content marketing is the practice of creating and distributing useful text, images, audio or video so that a defined audience pays attention to a company and, over time, takes actions that are good for the business, such as subscribing or buying. It works on the assumption that attention cannot be bought once and kept: it has to be earned again with every piece. The skill combines knowing an audience well, producing material that is worth their time, and connecting that material to a business goal.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Content marketing* is the strategic creation of content that delivers a relevant and engaging message to a customer or prospect and supports a business goal, including preparing the way for a sale. The Content Marketing Institute (CMI) defines it as "a strategic marketing approach focused on creating and distributing valuable, relevant, and consistent content to attract and retain a clearly defined audience and, ultimately, to drive profitable customer action" (checked 2026-10).

Three parts of the definition separate content marketing from things that look similar:

- **Value to the reader comes first.** An advertisement interrupts the reader to deliver the seller's message. A content piece is something the reader would choose to consume even if no product existed, for example a guide to folding shirts without wrinkles published by a shirt brand.
- **A defined audience.** The content is written for a specific group, not for "everyone".
- **A business purpose.** Content that serves no goal is publishing, not marketing. Content that serves only the goal and not the reader is advertising.

*Analogy.* Content marketing is like a cooking teacher who runs free classes in a kitchen shop. Students come for the class, not for the shop. Some of them later buy a knife because the teacher showed them why it matters. The analogy holds for the order of events: value first, sale later. It breaks in one place: in a class the teacher sees the students, while online most of the audience never identifies itself, so the marketer has to infer what works from data.

### The problem it solves

Before the web, a company that wanted attention mostly rented it: it paid a newspaper, a TV station or a mailing list owner for space in front of their audience. Paid attention stops when the payment stops. Search engines and social platforms made it possible for a company to become a publisher itself and to build an audience it reaches without paying for each contact. Content marketing is the discipline of doing that well.

### Audience-centric approach

The audience is wider than current buyers. It includes:

- **prospects**, people who might need the product,
- **leads**, prospects who have identified themselves, for example by subscribing,
- **customers**, who buy and can be kept,
- **influencers and advocates**, who do not necessarily buy but recommend.

Each group is at a different stage of its relationship with the company, so each needs different content. A prospect needs an answer to a problem. A customer needs help getting value from what they bought. An advocate needs something worth sharing.

### Earning attention

A company is not entitled to attention. The source notes put it this way: it must be earned through content that is engaging and valuable, and even "boring" fields such as dentistry or accounting are interesting to the people who need them. The techniques the source names are storytelling, humor and addressing the audience's frustrations directly. How attention is captured in practice is covered in [headlines](headlines.md) and [capturing attention](capturing-attention.md).

### Creativity and passion

Formulaic, "cookie-cutter" content is easy to produce and easy to ignore. The source notes advise finding people inside the organization (founders, salespeople, support staff) or among customers who care about the mission, and building content around what they know. If nobody in the organization cares, the lack of good content is a symptom of a deeper problem, not a writing problem.

### Role of the content marketer

The content marketer acts as the scribe or the voice of the organization. In practice this means:

- spending time with sales, support and product teams to collect real questions and stories,
- staying curious about subjects outside marketing, such as culture, technology or art, as a source of ideas and comparisons,
- combining professional writing (clear, accurate, on brand) with creative writing (voice, story, surprise).

### Owned, earned and paid media

A useful distinction from marketing practice is between *owned* media (channels the company controls, such as its website and email list), *earned* media (attention given by others, such as shares, reviews and press) and *paid* media (advertising). Content marketing is built on owned media, uses earned media to spread and may use paid media to start. The point of moving an audience to owned media is control: a platform can change its algorithm, but an email list stays yours. See the "content home base" idea in the [7A framework](seven-a-framework.md#attention).

### Core principles

- Earn attention rather than assume it.
- Serve every audience group, with content matched to its stage.
- Put real creativity and an authentic voice into each piece.
- Stay curious and draw material from many sources.

## Worked example

A small online shop sells loose leaf tea. Its audience is people who drink bagged tea and are curious about better tea.

1. **Audience and problem.** Support emails show that new customers often ask why their green tea tastes bitter.
2. **Content.** The shop writes a guide "Why green tea tastes bitter and how to fix it", explaining water temperature (about 70 to 80 °C for many green teas instead of boiling water), steeping time and leaf quantity. The guide is useful to someone who never buys from the shop.
3. **Business goal.** At the end, the guide offers a printable brewing chart in exchange for an email address. The email sequence teaches one tea per week and links to that tea in the shop.
4. **Measurement.** The shop tracks how many readers download the chart (conversion to lead) and how many leads buy within 60 days.

Check the example against the definition: the guide has value without the product, it is written for a defined audience, and it is connected to a goal that can be measured. If the guide were a list of the shop's products with prices, it would be an advertisement.

The brewing temperatures are common tea industry guidance and are given here for illustration only.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Slow to build.** Content usually needs months before search traffic or a subscriber base grows. It is a poor fit when a business needs sales next week. Paid advertising is faster and stops when the budget stops.
- **Compounding.** A useful article can bring readers for years, so the cost per visitor falls over time. Advertising cost per visitor does not fall in the same way.
- **Costs.** The real cost is skilled time: research, writing, editing, design and distribution.
- **Measurement is indirect.** A reader may read five articles over three months before buying. Attribution to a single piece is uncertain.
- **Wrong choice** when the product is a commodity bought only on price, when the audience does not search for information before buying, or when nobody in the company can contribute real expertise.
- **Search is changing.** When Google shows an AI summary above results, users click fewer links. In a 2025 Pew Research Center analysis of browsing data from 900 US adults, users clicked a result link on 8% of visits to pages with an AI summary and on 15% of visits to pages without one. Content that only repeats common answers is most exposed to this change.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing about the company instead of the reader.** Fix: start each piece from a question a real customer asked.
- **Publishing without a goal.** Fix: decide before writing what the reader should do next and how you will measure it. See [content marketing strategy](content-marketing-strategy.md).
- **Building only on rented platforms.** Fix: give readers a reason to join an owned channel, such as an email list.
- **Confusing volume with value.** Fix: publish less and make each piece the best answer you can find on its topic.
- **Formula content.** Fix: find people who care about the subject and use their knowledge and stories.
