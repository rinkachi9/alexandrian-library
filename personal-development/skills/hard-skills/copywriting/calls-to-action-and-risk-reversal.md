# Calls to action and risk reversal

## Summary

A call to action (CTA) is the instruction that tells the reader exactly what to do next, such as "Start your free trial". Risk reversal is everything that lowers the reader's fear of regret at that moment: guarantees, clear return policies, security signals and testimonials that answer specific worries. Together they form the last two steps of the five step formula in the source notes, which turns attention into a measurable result. Both work best when they are plain, visible, honest and tested.

## Prerequisites

[Copywriting fundamentals](copywriting-fundamentals.md) for the five step formula, and [persuasion principles](persuasion-principles.md) for the psychology behind it.

## Core concepts

### The five step formula

The source notes describe a formula for prompting action, based on direct response copywriting:

1. **Establish trust and authority.** Say who you are and why the reader should listen: experience, credentials, stories, testimonials, case studies.
2. **Describe the product or service.** Clear, concrete features, shown with images or examples where possible.
3. **Emphasize benefits, emotional and logical.** How the reader will feel, and the facts that justify the choice.
4. **Paint a picture of the transformation.** The "after" state: stories, case studies, before and after comparisons.
5. **Call to action.** One clear next step, supported by risk reversal.

The lesson in the source numbers the call to action and risk reversal as steps 4 and 5 in one place and step 5 in another. This note treats them as the closing pair of the formula.

### Call to action

*Definition.* A *call to action* is a short instruction, usually a button or a link, that names the next step: "Download the checklist", "Book a demo", "Add to cart". Its purpose is to bridge the gap between interest and action.

*Analogy.* A CTA is the exit sign in a building. People who have decided to leave look for it. If it is missing, hidden or ambiguous, they wander, and some give up. The analogy breaks because an exit sign never has to persuade anyone, while a CTA often has to remind the reader why the step is worth it.

Characteristics of a good CTA:

- **Clear and simple.** Plain wording beats clever wording. "Sign up free" says what happens. The source notes observe that even the old-fashioned "Click here" often performs well in tests. This is a practitioner observation, so test it in your setting.
- **Action and benefit.** Start with a verb and, where it fits, name the benefit: "Get the 10 minute budget template".
- **Visible.** Place it where the reader has enough information to decide, and repeat it on long pages, for example after the benefits and at the end.
- **One primary action.** Competing buttons split attention. Secondary actions can be links instead of buttons.
- **Consistent with the promise.** The page after the click must deliver what the CTA said.
- **Tested.** Compare wording and placement with an [A/B test](ab-testing.md), and track conversion rates with an analytics tool.

### Risk reversal

*Definition.* *Risk reversal* moves the risk of a bad decision from the buyer to the seller, or reduces it, so that the buyer's fear of losing money, time or face no longer blocks the decision.

The source notes describe this as addressing the "fear brain", the part of the mind that hesitates under uncertainty. The phrase is a metaphor. It is not a precise description of brain anatomy. The underlying idea is supported by research on loss aversion and on perceived risk in online buying, with the caveats in [persuasion principles](persuasion-principles.md#loss-aversion).

Common strategies:

- **Guarantees.** "30 day money-back guarantee" or "free replacement if it breaks in the first year". Place them close to the CTA.
- **Security signals.** Recognizable payment methods, an encrypted (HTTPS) checkout, and real certifications. Never show badges you are not entitled to.
- **Testimonials that answer fears.** "I wasn't sure it would work for a small team, but..." addresses a specific objection.
- **Transparent policies.** Short, clear refund, return and cancellation rules, linked from the checkout.
- **Answering objections.** Identify likely objections (price, time, effort, compatibility) and answer each one, for example in a short FAQ near the CTA. Free trials remove the cost of trying.

### Guarantees and the law

Some guarantees are required by law and cannot be sold as a special benefit. In the EU, consumers buying online generally have 14 days to withdraw from the contract without giving a reason (Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, with exceptions such as custom-made goods), and goods come with a legal guarantee of conformity of at least two years (Sale of Goods Directive (EU) 2019/771). Presenting a right the customer has anyway as if it were your extra offer can be a misleading practice under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. A guarantee that goes beyond the law, such as 60 days instead of 14, is a real risk reversal. This note is educational and is not legal advice.

### Testing and iteration

Testing comes from direct mail, where marketers sent different versions of a letter to different groups and counted replies. Online, the same idea is the A/B test. Start small: one page, one change (CTA wording, guarantee wording or position of testimonials), and a sample size decided in advance.

### Integrating the formula

- **Email sequences** can spread the elements over several emails: authority in the first, benefits in the second, a case study in the third, the offer with risk reversal in the fourth.
- **Sales pages** usually contain all five elements in order.
- **Content programs** can return to single elements regularly, for example a series of customer success stories.

### Ethics

Persuasion is not the same as manipulation. Ethical CTAs and guarantees inform the reader and help them make a decision that is right for them. A guarantee with hidden conditions, a cancellation that takes ten steps, or fake security badges destroy trust and can be illegal (see [dark patterns](persuasion-principles.md#ethics-and-dark-patterns)).

## Worked example

Three CTAs from the source notes, with risk reversal:

| Context | CTA | Risk reversal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online shop | "Add to cart, risk free with our 30 day money-back guarantee" | Recognizable payment logos and two customer reviews next to the button |
| Business software | "Schedule your free demo" | A testimonial from a company of similar size in the same industry |
| Non-profit | "Donate now" | A clear breakdown of how donations are used and a story from a beneficiary |

A worked test for the online shop. The shop shows the guarantee in small grey text below the button (version A) or in a short line inside the button area (version B). With a baseline conversion rate of 3%, detecting a 10% relative improvement needs about 52,000 visitors per version (see the calculation in [A/B testing](ab-testing.md#sample-size)). A shop with 2,000 visitors a week cannot run this test in a reasonable time. It should test bigger changes, such as a longer guarantee, or rely on principles and qualitative feedback.

Note on the guarantee: in the EU a 30 day money-back guarantee is better than the 14 day legal withdrawal right, so it is a real offer.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Generous guarantees raise trust and may raise refunds.** The net effect depends on the product. Measure both conversion and refund rate.
- **Free trials lower the barrier** but bring users who never intended to pay. Measure conversion from trial to paid.
- **More CTAs on a long page help readers who decide early,** but too many on a short page look pushy.
- **Small sites cannot test small changes.** Use tests for large differences only.

## Common mistakes

- **Vague CTAs** such as "Submit" or "Learn more" when a specific verb is possible. Fix: name the action and the benefit.
- **Hiding the guarantee in the terms.** Fix: put it next to the CTA.
- **Selling the legal minimum as a bonus.** Fix: state legal rights accurately and offer more if you can.
- **Fake trust badges.** Fix: show only real certifications.
- **Ending a test early because one version looks ahead.** Fix: fix the sample size in advance.
