# Social content creation

## Summary

Social content creation is planning and producing posts, videos and other formats that work in a feed: they stop the scroll in the first seconds, deliver something worth the viewer's time and invite a response. It rests on a few recurring content pillars tied to the audience and the business, on formats adapted to each platform, and on a production routine that makes consistency possible. Hooks, clear structure, native formats, captions and accessibility matter more than expensive equipment. Rights to music, images and the people shown must be cleared, and anything sponsored must be labeled.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Social content* is any material published on social platforms: short and long videos, images, carousels, text posts, stories, live streams, comments. *Content creation* is the whole process from idea to published post: planning, scripting, filming or designing, editing, writing captions and publishing.

*Analogy.* A social post is like a street performer's act. The performer has a few seconds to make passers-by stop, a few minutes to give them something worth stopping for, and a moment at the end to ask for a coin or a follow. The act is rehearsed many times, and the best performers know which tricks draw a crowd in their street. The analogy breaks because a street performer sees the crowd form and can adjust, while online the "crowd" is assembled by an algorithm based on how the first viewers react.

### Content pillars

*Content pillars* are three to five recurring themes that connect what the audience wants with what the business offers. For a running shoe shop: training tips, shoe reviews and comparisons, local running events, customer stories, behind the scenes. Pillars keep content focused and make planning easier. They are the social media equivalent of the pillars in a [content marketing strategy](../content-marketing/content-marketing-strategy.md).

### Types of posts by purpose

| Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Educate | Tips, how-tos, myth vs fact, mini tutorials |
| Entertain | Humor, trends adapted to the niche, behind the scenes |
| Inspire | Transformations, customer stories, achievements |
| Connect | Questions, polls, personal stories, team introductions |
| Promote | Products, offers, launches (a minority of posts) |

A common practitioner guideline is to keep promotional posts a minority (for example one in five). This is a convention, not a measured rule.

### The hook

The *hook* is the first line or first seconds that make someone stop. Effective hooks:

- show the result first (the finished cake, the before and after),
- state a surprising fact or a specific promise ("3 stretches that fix desk shoulders"),
- name the viewer ("If you run in the morning..."),
- open a curiosity gap that the post closes (see [headlines](../content-marketing/headlines.md#mechanism-benefit-and-curiosity)).

### Structure of a short video

1. **Hook** (0 to 3 seconds).
2. **Context or promise** (what the viewer will get).
3. **Content** in quick steps, cutting pauses.
4. **Payoff** (the result, the answer).
5. **Call to action** where relevant (follow, save, visit the link in the profile).

Text on screen and captions matter: many people watch without sound, and captions make video accessible to people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

### Writing for the feed

- First line works as a headline.
- Short paragraphs and line breaks for readability on phones.
- One idea per post.
- Specific numbers and examples.
- A question or prompt that invites genuine replies.
- Hashtags sparingly and relevantly, where the platform uses them for discovery.

### Native formats

Each platform has formats and conventions that the audience expects: vertical 9:16 video for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, document carousels on LinkedIn, square or vertical images on Instagram. Content made for one platform and posted unchanged on another (for example a video with another platform's watermark) usually performs worse. Adapt rather than copy (see [content formats and distribution](../content-marketing/content-formats-and-distribution.md#repurposing)).

### Production routine

Consistency requires a system:

1. **Idea bank:** a running list from customer questions, comments, trends and the content pillars.
2. **Batching:** plan, film and edit several pieces in one session.
3. **Templates:** recurring formats ("Myth Monday", "Tip of the week") that viewers recognize and that are faster to make.
4. **Calendar:** a simple schedule across platforms.
5. **Review:** monthly look at what worked (see [social media analytics](social-media-analytics.md)).

A phone, natural light, a simple microphone and an editing app are enough for most short video.

### Accessibility

- Captions on video.
- Alt text on images where the platform supports it.
- Readable text size and contrast.
- Camel case in hashtags (#MorningRun rather than #morningrun) so screen readers read them correctly.

### Rights and responsibilities

- **Music:** use the platform's licensed library for the account type. Business accounts often have restricted music libraries.
- **Images and footage:** use your own or licensed material.
- **People shown:** get consent, especially from customers and in any health or sensitive context. Images of identifiable people are personal data under the GDPR.
- **Claims:** consumer law applies to social posts (see [copywriting fundamentals](../copywriting/copywriting-fundamentals.md#legal-limits)).
- **Sponsored content** must be labeled (see [influencer marketing](influencer-marketing.md)).

## Worked example

A bicycle repair shop plans one month of content on Instagram and TikTok.

**Pillars:** quick fixes, maintenance by season, customer bikes, shop life.

**Batch session (3 hours):** film 8 short videos:

1. "Fix a slipping chain in 30 seconds" (hook: the chain slipping, then fixed).
2. "3 things to check before your first spring ride".
3. "Restoring a 1985 road bike, before and after" (customer bike, with permission).
4. "Why your brakes squeal" (myth vs fact).
5. to 8. variations of the formats above.

**Captions:** each opens with the promise, ends with a question ("What's the noise your bike makes that drives you mad?") and lists the shop's address in the profile.

**Accessibility:** captions burned into videos, alt text for images.

**Review after a month (invented numbers):** quick-fix videos averaged 4,200 views and many saves. Shop life videos averaged 600. Comments under quick-fix videos asked for more topics, which go into the idea bank. The shop doubles quick-fix content.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Polish versus speed.** High production can look like an ad and slow you down. Simple, authentic content often performs as well, depending on the brand.
- **Trends versus pillars.** Trends bring reach, pillars build a recognizable identity. Use trends only when they fit a pillar.
- **Quantity versus quality.** Posting more gives more chances to learn. Posting low-value content trains the audience to skip you.
- **Personal faces versus brand.** People connect with people. Depending on one person creates risk if they leave.

## Common mistakes

- **Slow openings.** Fix: lead with the result or the promise.
- **Cross-posting unchanged.** Fix: adapt to each platform's format.
- **No captions.** Fix: caption every video.
- **Unlicensed music and images.** Fix: use licensed libraries and own material.
- **All promotion.** Fix: give value in most posts.
