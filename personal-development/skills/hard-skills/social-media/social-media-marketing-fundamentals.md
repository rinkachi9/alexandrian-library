# Social media marketing fundamentals

## Summary

Social media marketing is the use of platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn and X to reach an audience, build relationships and lead people to actions that matter to a business or a professional. It combines organic work (posting content, talking with people, building a community) with paid advertising, and it works inside platforms whose algorithms decide who sees what. Its strengths are reach, targeting and two-way conversation. Its weaknesses are dependence on rules you do not control, declining organic reach for brand pages and difficulty measuring real business effect. A sound approach picks a few platforms where the audience actually is, publishes consistently, listens as much as it broadcasts and moves the relationship to owned channels.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Social media* are online platforms where users create, share and interact with content and with each other. *Social media marketing* is the planned use of these platforms to achieve business or professional goals: awareness, engagement, traffic, leads, sales, customer service, recruitment or reputation. It has two main modes:

- *Organic* social media: unpaid posts, comments, messages and community activity.
- *Paid* social media: advertising bought on the platforms (see [paid social advertising](paid-social-advertising.md)).

*Analogy.* Social media are like a very large set of town squares, each with its own culture. On one square people show photos, on another they debate, on another they talk about work. You can set up a stand (a profile), talk to people (organic posts and conversations) or pay the square's owner for a spot under the main lamp (ads). The square's owner decides where the crowd's attention goes, and can move your stand overnight. The analogy breaks because a real square shows everyone the same scene, while each social feed is different for every person, chosen by an algorithm.

### The problem it solves

Social platforms are where a large share of people spend their online time and discover new products, ideas and people. For a business they offer reach without a media budget, direct conversation with customers and precise targeting for ads. For a professional they offer visibility to colleagues, employers and clients (see [LinkedIn for professionals](linkedin-for-professionals.md)).

### Platforms differ

Each platform has its own audience, formats, culture and algorithm. A general picture (checked 2026-10, see [platforms and algorithms](platforms-and-algorithms.md)):

| Platform | Typical strengths | Main formats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | Search-driven video, tutorials, long attention | Long video, Shorts |
| Facebook | Broad adult audience, groups, local communities | Posts, groups, video, marketplace |
| Instagram | Visual products, lifestyle, creators | Reels, stories, carousels |
| TikTok | Discovery of new creators and products, younger adults | Short vertical video |
| LinkedIn | Professional audience, B2B, careers | Posts, articles, newsletters, company pages |
| X | News, real-time discussion, some professional niches | Short posts, threads |
| Pinterest | Planning and inspiration (home, food, fashion) | Pins linking to sites |

In the US, Pew Research Center surveys have found for years that YouTube and Facebook are the most widely used platforms among adults, with TikTok and Instagram more common among younger adults.

### Goals and the social media funnel

| Goal | What it looks like | Typical metrics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | People notice the brand or person | Reach, impressions, video views |
| Engagement | People react, comment, share, save | Engagement rate, comments, shares, saves |
| Traffic | People visit the website | Link clicks, sessions from social |
| Leads and sales | People sign up or buy | Conversions, cost per acquisition |
| Retention and service | Customers get help and stay | Response time, sentiment, repeat purchase |

A single account can serve several goals, but each piece of content should have one main purpose (see [social media analytics](social-media-analytics.md)).

### Organic reach and the role of algorithms

Feeds are ranked by algorithms that predict what each user is most likely to engage with. Brand pages typically reach only a fraction of their followers with each post, because they compete with friends, creators and ads. Platforms increasingly recommend content from accounts a user does not follow, based on interest, which gives new accounts a chance and makes follower counts less meaningful than they used to be.

### Listening, not only broadcasting

The source notes on the 7A framework describe social media as a listening tool first: a way to learn the audience's concerns, triumphs and needs (see [7A framework](../content-marketing/seven-a-framework.md#audience)). *Social listening* is the systematic monitoring of conversations about your brand, competitors and topics. It feeds content ideas, product improvements and early warning of problems.

### Owned versus rented audience

A following on a platform is *rented*: the platform owns the relationship and can change reach, rules or terms, or close an account. The 7A framework advises bringing the audience to a *content home base* you control, such as a website and an email list (see [email marketing](../content-marketing/email-marketing.md)).

### Strategy in brief

1. **Goal:** what social media should achieve, tied to the business.
2. **Audience:** who, and on which platforms they actually spend time.
3. **Platforms:** one or two to start, chosen by audience fit and the formats you can produce well.
4. **Content pillars:** three to five recurring themes (see [social content creation](social-content-creation.md)).
5. **Cadence:** what you can sustain.
6. **Community:** how you will respond and engage (see [community management](community-management.md)).
7. **Paid support:** whether and how to use ads.
8. **Measurement:** a few metrics per goal.

### Rules and responsibilities

Content must follow platform rules and the law: copyright for music and images, consumer law for claims and promotions, clear labeling of advertising and sponsored content (see [influencer marketing](influencer-marketing.md)), data protection when collecting leads, and rules on contests and giveaways that differ by country and platform.

## Worked example

A small physiotherapy clinic plans its social media.

- **Goal:** fill appointment slots with local patients and reduce questions by phone.
- **Audience:** adults 25 to 65 in the city, often with back or sports injuries.
- **Platforms:** Facebook (local groups and an older audience) and Instagram (younger, active patients). TikTok is postponed because the team cannot produce video weekly yet.
- **Content pillars:** short exercise tips from physiotherapists, explanations of common problems, behind the scenes at the clinic, patient stories (with written consent), practical information (prices, booking).
- **Cadence:** three posts a week, two stories a week.
- **Community:** reply to comments and messages within one working day, answer health questions generally and invite people to book for individual advice.
- **Paid support:** a small monthly ad budget promoting the booking page to people in the city.
- **Measurement:** bookings from the social link (tracked with campaign parameters), messages answered, reach among local users.

Health information on social media must be general and must not replace individual advice, which the clinic states in its posts.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Reach versus control.** Platforms give reach and take control. Build owned channels alongside.
- **Organic versus paid.** Organic builds trust slowly and cheaply in money but not in time. Paid gives speed and targeting at a cost.
- **Many platforms versus focus.** Being everywhere usually means being weak everywhere. Start with one or two.
- **Brand account versus people.** Content from real people (founders, employees, experts) often reaches and engages more than brand accounts, at the cost of depending on those people.
- **Not every business needs to be active everywhere.** For some B2B niches, a strong LinkedIn presence and email may be enough.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing platforms by trend instead of audience.** Fix: check where your audience actually spends time.
- **Broadcasting only.** Fix: listen, reply and take part in conversations.
- **Posting without a goal.** Fix: give each post a purpose and measure it.
- **Measuring only followers and likes.** Fix: track traffic, leads and sales where relevant.
- **Building everything on one platform.** Fix: move people to owned channels.
