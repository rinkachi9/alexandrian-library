# Platforms and algorithms

## Summary

Every social platform decides what each user sees with a recommendation system: software that predicts, from past behavior and the content itself, which posts a person is most likely to watch, like, comment on or share, and ranks the feed accordingly. Platforms publish general descriptions of these systems, and in the EU the Digital Services Act requires them to explain the main parameters and, for the largest ones, to offer a feed not based on profiling. The practical lessons are stable across platforms even though details change: content that holds attention and prompts genuine interaction is shown more, early reactions matter, and tricks to game the system tend to stop working. Choosing platforms is mainly a question of where your audience is and which formats you can produce well.

## Core concepts

### Definition

A *recommendation algorithm* (or ranking system) is a set of models that selects and orders content for a user's feed. It typically works in stages:

1. **Candidate generation:** collect thousands of possible posts (from followed accounts and from the wider platform).
2. **Ranking:** predict for each candidate the probability of actions (watch time, like, comment, share, save, hide, report) and combine them into a score.
3. **Filtering and integrity:** remove or demote content that breaks rules or is judged low quality.
4. **Diversity and freshness adjustments:** avoid showing too much of the same.

*Analogy.* A feed algorithm is like a waiter in a restaurant with an endless kitchen, who remembers everything you ever ate and how much of it you finished. Each time you sit down, the waiter brings the dishes you are most likely to finish and order again. A new dish gets a small tasting portion first, and if diners eat it eagerly, more of it goes out. The analogy breaks because a waiter has a sense of what is good for you, while an algorithm optimizes the signals it is given, which may not match your long-term interest, and is adjusted by the platform's commercial goals.

### Signals

Platforms describe several groups of signals:

- **User activity:** what you watched, liked, commented, shared, searched and how long you looked.
- **Relationships:** accounts you interact with often.
- **Content information:** captions, sounds, hashtags, topics recognized in the content, format.
- **Context:** device, language, location, time.
- **Negative feedback:** hiding, skipping quickly, "not interested", reports.

TikTok, for example, describes its "For You" recommendations as based on user interactions, video information and device and account settings. Meta publishes "system cards" in its Transparency Center that describe the signals used for Facebook and Instagram feeds, Reels and Explore. YouTube describes using watch time, likes and dislikes, and surveys of viewer satisfaction.

### What this means for content

- **The first seconds matter.** If people scroll past immediately, a post gets little further distribution. Start with the most interesting part.
- **Watch time and completion** are strong signals for video.
- **Meaningful interactions** (comments, shares, saves) are weighted more than passive views on many platforms.
- **Early response** gives a post a chance to be tested with larger audiences.
- **Interest graph over social graph.** Many platforms recommend content from accounts the user does not follow, based on interest. New accounts can reach large audiences with one strong post. Followers no longer guarantee reach.
- **Engagement bait is demoted.** Platforms say they demote posts that ask for likes or shares in manipulative ways ("comment YES if...").
- **Links out** are believed by many practitioners to receive less distribution on some platforms, because platforms prefer to keep users. Platforms do not consistently confirm this.

### Format choices

| Format | Strength | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short vertical video | Highest discovery on TikTok, Reels, Shorts | High (filming, editing), but can be simple |
| Image carousel | Saves and shares for tips and lists | Medium |
| Text post | Professional discussion on LinkedIn and X | Low |
| Long video | Search and depth on YouTube | High |
| Stories | Relationship with existing followers | Low |
| Live video | Real-time interaction, events | Medium |

### Transparency and choice under EU law

The Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) requires online platforms using recommender systems to set out in their terms the main parameters used and options to modify them (Article 27). Very large online platforms must offer at least one option not based on profiling (Article 38). This gives users in the EU a chronological or non-personalized feed option on major platforms.

### Choosing platforms

1. **Audience fit:** where your target group spends time, from surveys (for example the Pew Research Center's social media fact sheet for the US), your own customer survey and competitors' presence.
2. **Format fit:** which formats you can produce consistently and well.
3. **Goal fit:** search-driven platforms (YouTube, Pinterest) suit evergreen content and traffic. Discovery platforms (TikTok, Reels) suit reach. LinkedIn suits B2B and careers.
4. **Resources:** one platform done well beats four done poorly.

### Platform risk

Algorithms, formats and rules change without notice. Reach can drop overnight. Accounts can be restricted or closed, sometimes by mistake. Platforms may also be restricted by law in some countries. Diversify, back up your content, and keep an owned channel (see [email marketing](../content-marketing/email-marketing.md)).

## Worked example

A home baker sells cakes locally and tests two short videos on Instagram Reels (invented numbers).

| Video | First 3 seconds | Average watch time (of 30 s) | Shares | Reach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Logo and "Welcome to our bakery" | 4 s | 2 | 600 |
| B | Knife cutting a cake to show 8 layers | 19 s | 85 | 41,000 |

Video B starts with the most interesting moment, holds attention and gets shared, so the system shows it to many people who do not follow the account. The baker adapts: start every video with the result, keep videos under 30 seconds, put the shop's location in the caption and pinned comment rather than at the start of the video.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Optimizing for the algorithm versus the audience.** The two usually align (people engage with what they value), but chasing short-term engagement can push content toward sensationalism.
- **Discovery platforms bring reach, not always buyers.** Viral reach outside your area or niche may not convert.
- **Trends** give quick distribution and date fast.
- **Algorithm advice ages quickly.** Treat specific tips as hypotheses and test them.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing secret hacks.** Fix: focus on attention and genuine interaction.
- **Slow starts.** Fix: lead with the most interesting moment.
- **Engagement bait.** Fix: ask genuine questions.
- **Judging a format by one post.** Fix: test several posts per format.
- **Ignoring platform rules and the DSA rights of users.** Fix: know the rules of the platforms you use.
