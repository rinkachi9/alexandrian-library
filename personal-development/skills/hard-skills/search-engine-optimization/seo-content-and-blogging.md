# SEO content and blogging

## Summary

SEO content is content created to meet the needs behind specific search queries, so that it ranks and brings the right visitors over a long time. A company blog is the most common home for it. The approach that works is to pick topics from keyword research and customer questions, organize them into clusters around pillar pages, make each piece the most useful answer to its query, connect pieces with internal links, and keep them updated. Mass-produced, thin or AI-spun pages created only to rank are explicitly targeted by search engine spam policies, and simple answers are increasingly given directly on the results page, so original value matters more than ever.

[Keyword research](keyword-research.md), [on-page SEO](on-page-seo.md) and [content marketing fundamentals](../content-marketing/content-marketing-fundamentals.md).

## Core concepts

### Definition

*SEO content* is any content (articles, guides, comparisons, glossaries, tools, videos with transcripts) planned around the queries of a target audience and built to rank in search. *Blogging*, in the source notes, is described as a form of content marketing that uses on-page and technical SEO to increase a site's search visibility and credibility for specific keywords. Every published post has the potential to build reputation as a credible source, improve the site's authority and increase organic visibility.

*Analogy.* An SEO blog is like a well-stocked reference section in a shop. Each pamphlet answers one question customers often ask. Customers who come in for an answer see the products on the way out, and the shop becomes known as the place that knows. The analogy breaks because in a shop the pamphlets sit in one place, while online each article competes separately with every other answer to the same question.

### Choosing topics

Good topics sit where three things overlap:

1. **Search demand:** people search for it (see [keyword research](keyword-research.md)).
2. **Business relevance:** the topic is close to a problem your product solves.
3. **Ability to be the best answer:** you have experience, data or expertise others lack.

A simple prioritization: business relevance first, then realistic ranking chance, then volume.

### Topic clusters and pillar pages

A *pillar page* covers a broad subject comprehensively and links to *cluster pages* that cover subtopics in depth. Each cluster page links back to the pillar and to related clusters. Benefits:

- Readers can move from overview to detail.
- Internal links signal the structure and spread ranking signals.
- Each page targets a distinct intent, avoiding cannibalization.

```text
Pillar: Green tea guide (/guides/green-tea)
 ├── Why green tea tastes bitter (/guides/green-tea-bitter)
 ├── Sencha vs matcha (/guides/sencha-vs-matcha)
 ├── How to cold brew green tea (/guides/cold-brew-green-tea)
 ├── Green tea and caffeine (/guides/green-tea-caffeine)
 └── Choosing a teapot for green tea (/guides/green-tea-teapot)
```

### Writing a piece that ranks

1. **Study the SERP.** What format, depth and angle do the top results have? What do they all miss?
2. **Outline** around the reader's sub-questions ("People also ask", customer emails).
3. **Answer first,** then expand (see [writing for the web](../copywriting/writing-for-the-web.md)).
4. **Add original value:** first-hand testing, photos, data, expert quotes, worked examples.
5. **Show who wrote it** and why they know: author name, experience, sources.
6. **Optimize** title, headings, URL, images and structured data (see [on-page SEO](on-page-seo.md)).
7. **Link** to the pillar, related pieces and relevant products.
8. **Present the product as a solution** where it genuinely helps, as the source notes advise: not every post must be about products, but linking to a product page or two when appropriate is fine.

### Maintenance and updates

Content decays: facts change, competitors publish better pages, and rankings slip. A *content refresh* updates facts, adds new information, improves structure and keeps the same URL. A *content audit* reviews all pages periodically and decides for each one: keep, update, merge with another page (and redirect), or remove. Many sites gain more from refreshing and merging existing pages than from publishing new ones. This is practitioner experience, not a measured rule.

### AI-generated content

Google's guidance states that it rewards helpful content however it is produced, and that using automation, including generative AI, primarily to manipulate rankings violates its spam policies (*scaled content abuse*, since March 2024). In practice:

- AI tools can help with outlines, summaries and drafts.
- Facts, citations and claims must be checked by a person (see [research with AI tools](../online-research/research-with-ai-tools.md)).
- Mass-producing pages on topics where you have nothing original to add is both a policy risk and pointless, because such pages add nothing to what already exists and to what AI summaries already show.

### Content and AI summaries

Search engines increasingly answer simple informational queries directly. Pages most exposed are those that give short, generic answers. Content that holds value includes: original data and testing, opinionated expert analysis, tools, detailed comparisons with first-hand experience, and content that serves a buying decision on your own site.

## Worked example

A small accounting firm for freelancers plans a blog.

1. **Customer questions** from emails and calls: "Can I deduct my laptop?", "When do I pay quarterly tax?", "Lump sum or tax scale?"
2. **Keyword research** confirms search demand for each, with commercial intent rising near tax deadlines.
3. **Cluster.** Pillar: "Taxes for freelancers: a complete guide". Clusters: deductible expenses, payment deadlines calendar, choosing a tax form (with a calculator), VAT registration thresholds.
4. **Original value.** The calculator lets readers compare tax forms with their own numbers. Each article includes anonymized examples from the firm's practice.
5. **Business link.** Each article ends with "Want us to check this for your case? Book a 20 minute call."
6. **Maintenance.** Every January, the firm updates thresholds, rates and deadlines, and notes the date checked at the top of each article.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Long-term investment.** Blogs pay off after months and keep paying if maintained.
- **Breadth versus depth.** Many shallow posts dilute quality. Fewer, better posts in clusters usually perform better.
- **Topical fit.** Ranking for unrelated popular topics brings traffic that never buys and may dilute how search engines understand the site.
- **Exposure to AI answers.** Simple informational posts are losing clicks. Invest in content that is hard to replace.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing about what interests you, not what customers search.** Fix: start from questions and keyword research.
- **No internal links.** Fix: build clusters and link deliberately.
- **Publishing and forgetting.** Fix: schedule content audits.
- **Mass-produced AI content.** Fix: use AI as an assistant and add original value.
- **No path to the business.** Fix: end each piece with a relevant next step.
