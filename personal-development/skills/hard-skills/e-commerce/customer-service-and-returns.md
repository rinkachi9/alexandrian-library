# Customer service and returns

## Summary

Customer service in e-commerce is all contact with buyers before and after purchase: answering questions, solving delivery problems, handling complaints, returns and refunds. Online buyers cannot touch the product or talk to a shop assistant, so service and clear policies replace much of the trust a physical shop provides, and good handling of a problem can keep a customer who would otherwise leave. In the EU, consumers have a legal 14-day right to withdraw from online purchases and a legal guarantee of at least two years, so part of "returns policy" is not a choice. Returns are a real cost and must be planned into prices and processes.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Customer service* is the support a business gives customers before, during and after a purchase. *Returns management* (reverse logistics) is the process of receiving products back from customers, checking them, refunding or replacing, and deciding what happens to the returned goods. A *complaint* (in EU terms often a claim under the legal guarantee) concerns a defective or non-conforming product, while a *return under the right of withdrawal* needs no reason.

*Analogy.* Customer service is the shop assistant of an online store, and the returns policy is the shop's promise on the wall. In a physical shop, a friendly assistant who swaps a faulty item without fuss makes customers return for years. Online, the assistant is an email, a chat window or a phone line, and the promise must be written clearly because nobody can ask the person behind the counter. The analogy breaks because online returns cost the seller a two-way shipment and handling, which a physical shop's counter swap does not.

### Before the purchase

Pre-sale questions reveal what the product page fails to explain. Useful practices:

- an FAQ built from real questions,
- clear information on sizes, materials, compatibility, delivery and returns on the product page,
- fast answers on chat or email during business hours,
- turning repeated questions into page improvements (see [customer research](../copywriting/customer-research.md)).

### After the purchase

- **Order updates:** confirmation, shipping notification with tracking, delivery confirmation.
- **Delivery problems:** delays, damaged or lost parcels. The seller deals with the customer and then with the carrier or supplier.
- **Product questions:** how to use, care, assemble.
- **Complaints and returns.**

The source notes stress that even in dropshipping, where the supplier ships, the seller is responsible for customer service, including queries, complaints and returns.

### Service standards

Define measurable targets, for example:

| Metric | Meaning | Example target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First response time | Time until the first reply | Under 4 business hours |
| Resolution time | Time until the issue is solved | Under 2 business days |
| First contact resolution | Share solved in the first reply | As high as possible |
| Customer satisfaction (CSAT) | Rating after the interaction | Track the trend |
| Contact rate | Contacts per 100 orders | Falling over time as pages improve |

Example targets are illustrative.

### The service recovery idea

The *service recovery paradox* is the claim that customers whose problem is solved well can end up more satisfied and loyal than customers who never had a problem. Meta-analyses have found mixed evidence: some find a positive effect on satisfaction but not on repurchase intentions or loyalty, and the effect depends on the severity of the failure and the quality of the recovery (de Matos, Henrique and Rossi, 2007). The safe lesson is narrower: when something goes wrong, fast and fair recovery limits the damage. It does not justify failing on purpose.

### Legal framework in the EU

- **Right of withdrawal.** Consumers buying at a distance generally have 14 days from receiving the goods to withdraw without giving a reason (Directive 2011/83/EU). The seller must refund the payment, including the cost of standard delivery, within 14 days of being informed, and may withhold the refund until the goods are received back or proof of return is provided. The consumer pays the direct cost of returning the goods unless the seller agreed to pay it or did not inform the consumer that they must. Exceptions include goods made to the consumer's specifications, perishable goods and sealed hygiene products that were unsealed.
- **Information duties.** The seller must inform about the right of withdrawal and provide the model withdrawal form. If not informed, the withdrawal period extends by up to 12 months.
- **Legal guarantee of conformity.** Goods must conform to the contract. The seller is liable for lack of conformity that becomes apparent within at least two years of delivery (Directive (EU) 2019/771). The consumer is entitled to repair or replacement and, in some cases, a price reduction or termination.
- **Commercial guarantees** offered by the seller or manufacturer are voluntary and add to, not replace, the legal guarantee.

US rules differ: there is no general federal right to return online purchases. Return rights depend on the seller's policy and state law, and the FTC's mail order rule covers shipping delays (see [e-commerce law and cross-border](e-commerce-law-and-cross-border.md)).

This note is educational and is not legal advice.

### Returns economics

Returns cost shipping (often both ways), handling, inspection, repackaging, and sometimes the loss of the item's value. Return rates vary strongly by category: clothing and shoes have high rates because of fit, while consumables have low rates. Ways to reduce returns without restricting legal rights:

- precise size guides, measurements and fit information,
- accurate photos and descriptions, including what the product is not,
- reviews that mention fit and quality,
- asking for the return reason and fixing the cause.

Some retailers have started charging for returns beyond the legal minimum or for returns by certain channels. Within the EU, the consumer can be asked to bear the direct cost of returning if informed in advance.

### Returns process

1. Customer requests a return (form or email).
2. The seller confirms and provides instructions or a label.
3. Item received and inspected.
4. Refund or replacement within the legal deadline.
5. Item restocked, refurbished, sold as returned stock, donated or recycled.
6. Return reason recorded and analyzed.

## Worked example

A clothing shop has a 28% return rate (invented numbers). Return reasons recorded for three months:

| Reason | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Too small | 34% |
| Too large | 21% |
| Not as expected (color, material) | 18% |
| Changed mind | 15% |
| Defective | 4% |
| Other | 8% |

**Actions.**

1. Add garment measurements in centimeters for each size, not only S, M, L.
2. Add "fits small, consider one size up" notes where reviews and return data agree.
3. Photograph each product in daylight and include a close-up of the fabric.
4. Keep the legal 14-day withdrawal right, explain it clearly, and offer free exchanges for size to encourage exchanges over refunds.

**Result after three months:** return rate falls to 23%, mostly fewer "too small" returns. Changes in the product mix may also contribute.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Generous policies** (longer return windows, free returns) increase trust and conversion and raise return costs. Measure both.
- **Fast service versus cost.** Staffing for instant replies is expensive. Self-service answers (FAQ, order tracking page) handle many questions.
- **Automation.** Chatbots and templates speed up answers and frustrate customers when they block access to a person.
- **Return fees** reduce returns and can reduce conversion.

## Common mistakes

- **Hard-to-find return information.** Fix: link it from product pages and the checkout.
- **Refusing legal rights.** Fix: know the 14-day withdrawal and two-year guarantee rules.
- **Treating complaints as costs only.** Fix: record reasons and fix causes.
- **Slow refunds.** Fix: refund within the legal deadline and say when.
- **Blaming the supplier to the customer.** Fix: own the problem and solve it.
