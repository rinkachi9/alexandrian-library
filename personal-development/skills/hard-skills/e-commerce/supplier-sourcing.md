# Supplier sourcing

## Summary

Supplier sourcing is finding, evaluating and contracting the businesses that make or supply your products. For an online seller it decides product quality, cost, delivery time and legal compliance, and so most of what customers experience. The process runs from defining requirements, through searching marketplaces, trade fairs and directories, to shortlisting, sampling, negotiating terms (price, minimum order quantity, lead time, payment, Incoterms) and checking quality before shipment. The source notes describe the dropshipping version of the process. The same principles apply, with higher stakes, to buying stock and to private label manufacturing.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*Sourcing* is the process of identifying, evaluating and selecting suppliers and agreeing terms with them. A *supplier* may be a manufacturer, a wholesaler, a distributor or a dropshipping provider. *Procurement* is the broader term that also covers ordering, receiving and paying.

*Analogy.* Sourcing a supplier is like hiring a key employee you will rarely see. You check references, give a trial task (samples), agree clear terms and keep checking the work, because your customers will judge you by what that person delivers. The analogy breaks because an employee works under your direct supervision and law, while a supplier is often in another country, under other law, which makes contracts, inspections and payment terms your main controls.

### Types of suppliers

| Type | What they do | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacturer | Produces the goods | Private label, larger orders, custom products |
| Wholesaler or distributor | Buys from manufacturers, sells in bulk | Stocking known brands or generic goods |
| Dropshipping supplier | Stores and ships single orders to your customers | Testing products, small stores |
| Trading company or agent | Intermediary that sources from factories | When you cannot deal with factories directly |
| Local craftsperson or small producer | Small batches, often handmade | Niche and premium products |

### Where to find suppliers

- **B2B marketplaces:** Alibaba (international), Global Sources, Made-in-China, and regional directories. The source notes list AliExpress, As Seen On TV and Wish for finding products, and AliExpress, Zendrop, Spocket and dropshipping.com as dropshipping suppliers.
- **Trade fairs:** for example the Canton Fair in Guangzhou, Ambiente in Frankfurt and industry-specific fairs. Meeting suppliers in person reveals much more than online profiles.
- **Industry associations and chambers of commerce.**
- **Local manufacturers,** found through directories, LinkedIn or by asking other sellers.
- **Sourcing agents** who search and negotiate for a fee or commission.

### Requirements before searching

Write a short *product specification*: materials, dimensions, weight, colors, tolerances, packaging, labeling, certifications needed (for example CE marking in the EU), target unit cost and order quantities. Without it, quotes are not comparable and samples cannot be judged.

### Evaluating suppliers

| Criterion | How to check |
| --- | --- |
| Legitimacy | Business license, company registration, years in business, address (verified on video call or visit) |
| Capability | Factory photos and videos, production capacity, similar products made |
| Quality | Samples, quality certifications (for example ISO 9001), inspection reports, reviews |
| Compliance | Test reports for your market, ability to provide documentation |
| Communication | Response time, clarity, English or your language |
| Terms | Price, minimum order quantity, lead time, payment terms, defect policy |

The source notes stress choosing suppliers who offer quality products, timely delivery and good service, and building a relationship with good communication and prompt resolution of issues.

### Samples

Order samples from several shortlisted suppliers and compare them side by side against the specification. For private label, approve a *golden sample* (the reference that production must match) and keep it. The source notes give the range of sample costs for custom products as anywhere from 50 to 100,000 dollars, because custom tooling may be needed.

### Key commercial terms

- **Unit price** at different quantities.
- **MOQ** (minimum order quantity) per order and per variant.
- **Lead time:** production time plus shipping.
- **Payment terms:** common international terms are a deposit (often 30%) before production and the balance before shipment, or payment through an escrow or trade assurance service. Letters of credit are used for larger orders.
- **Incoterms:** standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (current edition Incoterms 2020) that define who pays for and bears the risk of transport, insurance and customs at each point. For example, *EXW* (Ex Works) means the buyer handles everything from the factory door, *FOB* (Free On Board) means the seller delivers the goods loaded on the vessel at the named port, and *DDP* (Delivered Duty Paid) means the seller delivers to the buyer's destination with duties paid.
- **Defects and returns:** what happens if goods do not match the specification.
- **Intellectual property:** for private label, who owns molds and designs. Agreements such as NNN (non-disclosure, non-use, non-circumvention) are used with Chinese manufacturers to protect designs.

### Quality control

- **Pre-production check** of materials.
- **During production inspection** for large orders.
- **Pre-shipment inspection** by an independent inspection company, using sampling plans based on *AQL* (acceptance quality limit) according to ISO 2859-1. An AQL inspection checks a statistically chosen sample and accepts or rejects the batch based on the number of defects found.

### Dropshipping process from the source notes

1. Research and select reliable suppliers (local or international, depending on the model).
2. Choose products that fit the brand and the audience.
3. Negotiate terms: prices, delivery times, return policies.
4. List the products with detailed descriptions, good images and competitive prices.
5. When an order comes in, pass it to the supplier, who packs and ships to the customer.
6. Handle customer service, including questions, complaints and returns.

The source adds that a product that can be bought anywhere will not stand out. You must offer value by solving a problem or improving customers' lives.

## Worked example

A seller sources 500 bamboo cutting boards for a private label kitchen brand (invented numbers).

1. **Specification:** 30 × 20 × 1.5 cm, food-safe finish, laser-engraved logo, kraft box, compliance with EU food contact material rules, documentation available.
2. **Search:** 12 suppliers on a B2B marketplace, filtered by experience with bamboo kitchenware and exports to the EU.
3. **Shortlist:** 5 suppliers after video calls, factory photos and a request for test reports.
4. **Samples:** 3 suppliers send samples (40 to 60 dollars each including courier). One warps after a dishwasher test (the specification says hand wash, but customers will try). One has a strong smell. One passes.
5. **Negotiation:** unit price 4.20 dollars at 500 units FOB Shanghai, 30% deposit, 70% after inspection, lead time 25 days, defects above AQL 2.5 to be replaced at the supplier's cost.
6. **Inspection:** a third-party pre-shipment inspection finds defects within the limit. Balance paid, goods shipped.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **Cheapest versus reliable.** The lowest quote often hides lower quality or poor communication. Total cost includes defects, delays and returns.
- **Overseas versus local.** Overseas manufacturing is often cheaper per unit, local manufacturing is faster, easier to control and more flexible with small orders.
- **One supplier versus several.** One supplier is simpler and gives more negotiating weight. A backup supplier protects against delays and failures.
- **Inspection costs** are small compared with the cost of a bad batch.

## Common mistakes

- **No written specification.** Fix: specify before asking for quotes.
- **Skipping samples or comparing only one.** Fix: sample several suppliers.
- **Paying everything up front without protection.** Fix: deposits, escrow or trade assurance, and payment of the balance after inspection.
- **Unclear Incoterms.** Fix: state the Incoterm and place in every quote and contract.
- **Forgetting compliance.** Fix: request test reports for your market before ordering.
