# E-commerce law and cross-border selling

## Summary

An online shop is a business under consumer protection, data protection, tax, product safety and accessibility law, and selling to other countries adds their rules, plus VAT and customs. In the EU the core consumer rules are harmonized: pre-contract information, a 14-day right of withdrawal, a two-year legal guarantee, transparent prices and a ban on misleading practices. Since 2021 VAT is due on all imported goods, since July 2026 a flat customs duty applies to low-value parcels, and since 2025 the United States no longer exempts small imported parcels from duty. This note gives an educational overview so that a seller knows which questions to ask. It is not legal or tax advice.

## Core concepts

### Definition

*E-commerce law* is the set of legal rules that apply to selling goods and services online: contract formation, consumer rights, information duties, data protection, payment rules, product safety, accessibility, taxation and customs. *Cross-border e-commerce* is selling to customers in another country, which brings in the rules of that country.

*Analogy.* Selling online across borders is like driving a delivery van through several countries. The van is the same, but each country has its own speed limits, tolls and paperwork, and the police of the country you are in apply their rules, not yours. Within the EU many signs are harmonized, which makes the trip easier, but tolls (VAT rates) still differ. The analogy breaks because online you can "drive" into a country without noticing, as soon as a customer there orders, so you must decide in advance where you sell and how.

### EU consumer rules for online sales

| Rule | Main source | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-contract information | Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU | Identity and contact of the trader, main characteristics, total price with taxes and delivery costs, payment and delivery terms, right of withdrawal, legal guarantee |
| Order button | Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 8(2) | The button that places the order must state clearly that it involves an obligation to pay |
| Right of withdrawal | Directive 2011/83/EU | 14 days without reason, refund within 14 days, exceptions |
| Delivery | Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 18 | Within the agreed time or within 30 days if none agreed |
| Legal guarantee | Directive (EU) 2019/771 | At least two years for lack of conformity |
| Fair practices | Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC | Ban on misleading and aggressive practices, fake reviews, fake scarcity |
| Price reductions | Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC as amended by Directive (EU) 2019/2161 | A price reduction must be shown against the lowest price in the previous 30 days |
| Personalized prices | Directive 2011/83/EU as amended | Inform consumers when a price was personalized by automated decision-making |
| Geo-blocking | Regulation (EU) 2018/302 | No unjustified discrimination by nationality or residence in access to online stores |

Member states implement directives in national law, sometimes with stricter rules. In Poland the main act is the Consumer Rights Act (*ustawa o prawach konsumenta*).

### Data protection and privacy

- **GDPR** (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) applies to all processing of personal data of customers and visitors: legal basis, transparency (privacy notice), data minimization, security, data subject rights, processors' contracts.
- **ePrivacy rules** (Directive 2002/58/EC, Article 5(3)) require consent for storing or reading information on users' devices, such as non-essential cookies for analytics and advertising.
- **Electronic marketing** generally needs prior consent (see [email marketing](../content-marketing/email-marketing.md#consent-and-law)).

### Product safety and compliance

- **General Product Safety Regulation** (Regulation (EU) 2023/988), applying since 13 December 2024: products must be safe. Many products need an economic operator established in the EU responsible for them. Online listings must show the manufacturer, the responsible person, product identification and warnings. Online marketplaces have specific duties.
- **Sector rules:** CE marking under product-specific legislation (electrical equipment, toys, machinery and others), cosmetics, food and food contact materials, chemicals (REACH), batteries, packaging and waste electrical equipment, with national registration duties.
- **Manufacturer status:** a business that sells under its own name or brand is generally treated as the manufacturer (see [private label](private-label.md)).

### Accessibility

The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) applies since 28 June 2025 to e-commerce services offered to consumers, requiring websites and apps to be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust for people with disabilities. Microenterprises providing services (fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding 2 million euros) are exempt from the service requirements. The technical reference is the harmonized standard EN 301 549, which builds on WCAG.

### Platforms and marketplaces

The Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) sets duties for online platforms, including marketplaces: traceability of traders (they must collect and verify identification of sellers), notice and action for illegal content and products, and a ban on manipulative interface design (dark patterns, see [persuasion principles](../copywriting/persuasion-principles.md#ethics-and-dark-patterns)). Sellers on marketplaces must provide accurate identification.

### VAT on cross-border sales

- **Within the EU (B2C).** Since 1 July 2021, distance sales of goods to consumers in other EU countries are taxed at the VAT rate of the customer's country once a seller's total cross-border sales exceed 10,000 euros a year. The *One-Stop Shop* (OSS) lets the seller declare and pay this VAT in one country.
- **Imports into the EU.** The exemption from import VAT for goods worth up to 22 euros was abolished on 1 July 2021. The optional *Import One-Stop Shop* (IOSS) lets sellers of consignments worth up to 150 euros collect VAT at checkout. Non-EU sellers generally need an EU intermediary. Marketplaces can be deemed suppliers for VAT on certain sales.
- **Customs duty on low-value imports into the EU.** Goods worth up to 150 euros were exempt from customs duty. Since 1 July 2026 a temporary flat duty of 3 euros applies per item category in a low-value consignment, until the exemption is removed as part of the EU customs reform (expected around 2028).

### Selling to the United States

- **Consumer rules:** the FTC Act prohibits deceptive practices. The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR Part 435) requires a reasonable basis for stated shipping times, shipping within 30 days if no time is stated, and an option to accept a delay or cancel for a prompt refund. The FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) has been in force since 21 October 2024. Many rules are state-level, including sales tax collection obligations that depend on a seller's sales into each state.
- **De minimis:** the US exempted imports worth up to 800 dollars from duty. The exemption was suspended for goods from China and Hong Kong from 2 May 2025 and for all countries from 29 August 2025 (Executive Order 14324), so duties now apply to small parcels.

### Checklist before selling to a new country

1. Consumer law: withdrawal, guarantee, information, language requirements.
2. VAT or sales tax registration and rates.
3. Customs and duties for your products and origin.
4. Product compliance: certifications, labeling in the local language, responsible person.
5. Payment methods and currency.
6. Delivery options, times and costs, including returns.
7. Data protection and cookies.
8. Accessibility, where applicable.

Checked 2026-10. These rules change often. Verify current versions before relying on them.

## Worked example

A Polish shop selling handmade candles plans to sell to Germany and the United States.

**Germany (EU).**

- Consumer law: German-language pre-contract information and withdrawal instructions, model withdrawal form, order button text in German with equivalent meaning to "order with obligation to pay".
- VAT: the shop's sales to other EU countries are expected to exceed 10,000 euros, so it registers for OSS in Poland and charges German VAT (19%) on German orders.
- Product compliance: candles must be safe under the GPSR, with appropriate warning labels in German. The shop is the manufacturer.
- Packaging: register for packaging under the German packaging law, as required for sellers placing packaging on the German market.
- Delivery: realistic times shown before ordering.

**United States.**

- No de minimis exemption since August 2025, so the shop must decide whether to ship with duties paid (DDP) or let the customer pay duties on delivery, and show this clearly.
- Shipping times must have a reasonable basis under the FTC mail order rule.
- Sales tax: check state thresholds for remote sellers. With small volumes, the obligation may not yet arise in most states.

The steps are illustrative and must be checked with an adviser for a real business.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- **More markets mean more revenue and more compliance work.** Enter one market at a time and check rules before launch.
- **Marketplaces handle some obligations** (VAT as deemed supplier in some cases, payment) but not all (product safety, your own information duties).
- **Advisers cost money,** but errors in VAT and product compliance can cost more.
- **Overcompliance** in generous policies (longer returns) can be a sales argument, as long as legal minimums are not presented as extras.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying another shop's terms.** Fix: draft terms that match your actual processes and law.
- **Presenting legal rights as a bonus.** Fix: describe them accurately.
- **Ignoring VAT thresholds.** Fix: track cross-border sales and register for OSS when required.
- **Selling non-compliant products.** Fix: check product rules before listing.
- **Assuming US rules match EU rules.** Fix: check each market separately.
