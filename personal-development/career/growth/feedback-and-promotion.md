# Feedback and promotion

## Summary

Feedback is information about how your performance compares to a standard, and it does not always help. A large review of feedback interventions found that on average they improve performance, but that a substantial share of them make it worse, depending on where the feedback directs attention. Advancement rests on visible results, clear expectations and people who know your work. The practical approach is to ask for specific feedback on tasks, keep a record of results and agree what the next level requires.

### Promotion

There is little research in this note on promotion mechanics, so this section is practitioner advice and is marked as such.

- Agree what the next level requires, in writing if possible.
- Keep an evidence log of results with dates and numbers.
- Make your work visible to people who decide, through reports, demos and short updates.
- Ask a senior person to advocate for you (sponsorship), as an extension of [mentoring](../methods/mentoring-process-and-relationships.md).

## Worked example

A three-question feedback request after a project (synthetic):

1. "What is one thing in the report that helped you decide?"
2. "What is one thing that slowed you down?"
3. "What should I do differently on the next project?"

An evidence log entry:

| Date | Result | Evidence | Who saw it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-03 | Cut report time from 3 days to 1 | Timesheets | Team lead, finance |

After six months you have a dozen entries. In the review meeting, match them to the expectations for the next level. The entries and numbers are examples.

## Trade offs and when to use it

- Frequent requests can look insecure if they are vague. Specific, task-based questions look professional.
- A log takes minutes per week and saves hours before reviews.
- A promotion is not the only way to grow. A lateral move can build skills faster. See [career planning process](../planning/career-planning-process.md).

## Common mistakes

- Taking feedback as a verdict on yourself. Fix: separate the task from the person.
- Waiting for the annual review. Fix: ask after each project.
- Assuming good work is seen automatically. Fix: summarize results for the people who decide.
